# 山海食肆文化读本 · Chinese & English

修订：2026-10-29。30位员工、25道菜品、6位来客。中文与英文分别润写，原文节选配中文白话与英文释意。

欢迎页或系统页可切换中文/English；来客小传仍须遇见后阅读。英文典籍节选标明译者或本项目译述，原文与美术替换通道保留。

## 山海伙伴

### 羬羊 · Qián yáng

羬羊从钱来山来，羊身后拖着马尾，如今守着小馆的汤灶。它话少，尝汤时却格外挑剔，连一粒浮盐也逃不过舌尖。

**松下有客**

《西山经》从钱来山起笔：山上多松，山下多石，松影里走出一只羊，身后竟是一条马尾。羬羊的奇处藏得安静，像山路转弯后才看见的风景。认得这条尾巴，也就认得了山海开篇的一位邻居。

**典籍里的身世**

钱来山不仅有松林，还多“洗石”，郭璞把它解释为能洗濯去垢的石。羬羊就在这些与日常起居相连的风物间出场，脂膏又被古人赋予润护肌肤的用途。郭璞注进一步想到西域大月氏的大羊：体大如驴，也拖着马尾；又引《尔雅》“羊六尺为羬”。山兽的奇貌、远方见闻与古人的畜牧知识，由此汇进同一个名字。再看小馆灶边那条尾巴，便仿佛还跟着一阵西来的长风。

**汤开以前**

它不爱旁人掀锅盖，添一瓢水也要问清缘由。可晚归的伙计若错过饭点，灶上总还温着一碗汤。有人说它小气，羬羊便把汤勺一放：“盐放重了，谁替这一锅水可惜？”

**一勺清鲜**

江南清汤、远方家乡的炖锅，都能把赶路的人留住。羬羊常请初来的客人先喝一小口，再找汤里的鲜。这个字在舌尖展开时，松林与炉火便有了同一种温度。

钱来山条从山中物产写到羬羊，把它安放在一幅完整的山景里。

> 西山經華山之首曰錢來之山，其上多松，其下多洗石。有獸焉，其狀如羊而馬尾，名曰羬羊，其脂可以已腊。
> ——《山海经·西山经》·钱来之山
> The first of the Hua mountains in the Western Mountains is Mount Qianlai. Pines abound on its heights, and polishing stones at its foot. There is an animal there shaped like a sheep, but with a horse's tail. It is called Qianyang; its fat can heal chapped skin.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Western Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

《西山经》所记华山山系的第一座山叫钱来山，山上多松，山下多可供洗濯的石。有兽形似羊而长着马尾，名叫羬羊；古书还记下以它的脂膏缓解皮肤皴裂的说法。

郭璞把这个名字接向西域见闻，又借《尔雅》解释“羬”字。

> 今大月氐國有大羊如驢而馬尾。爾雅云羊六尺為羬，謂此羊也。
> ——《山海经·西山经》·郭璞注
> In the country of the Great Yuezhi today there are large sheep, as big as donkeys, with horses' tails. The Erya says, 'A sheep six chi tall is a qian.' It is this sheep that is meant.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Western Mountains · Guo Pu commentary

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

郭璞说，西域大月氏国有体大如驴、长着马尾的羊；他又引《尔雅》“羊六尺为羬”，认为说的就是这类羊。

**Qianyang, the Mountain Sheep**

Qianyang keeps the soup stove, its horse's tail betraying a visitor from Qianlai Mountain. Quiet in company, it is exacting with a tasting spoon.

**Under the pines**

The Western Mountains opens at Qianlai: pines above, stone below, and a sheep-shaped creature with a horse's tail among them. That unexpected tail gives the mountain a memorable inhabitant. It is a small, vivid doorway into the Classic of Mountains and Seas.

**A life in the old books**

Qianlai contains pines and washing stones, which Guo Pu explains as stones used to cleanse the body. The account also attributes a skin-soothing use to Qianyang's fat. Guo's commentary looks westward: great sheep in the land of the Yuezhi were said to resemble donkeys in size and carry horse-like tails. He connects them with the Erya's description of a sheep of six chi as qian. Mountain marvel, husbandry and distant observation gather within one name, giving the little inn's cook a much wider horizon.

**Before the pot boils**

Qianyang dislikes anyone lifting its lids. Yet a bowl always stays warm for the worker who missed supper. Ask why it guards the seasoning so fiercely and the spoon comes down: every pot deserves better than a careless handful of salt.

**A spoonful of xian**

Guests bring memories of clear soups and slow stews from home. Qianyang asks them to sip, then follow xian: the savoury freshness opening beneath the steam. A Chinese word begins to acquire a flavour.

### 鸮 · Xiāo

鸮是夜市里的服务员。白天它总像没睡醒，灯一亮，那双圆眼便能从满堂人影里认出一只即将空掉的茶杯。

**梅树上的夜声**

《诗经》写“墓门有梅，有鸮萃止”，把鸮留在树影与暮色之间。夜鸟的声音曾让古人想到幽暗、孤寂，也让长夜有了可辨认的回声。它带来的，是中国诗歌里一双看惯黑暗的眼睛。

**典籍里的身世**

鸮的来路横跨诗歌与训诂。《尔雅·释鸟》把鸱鸮列入鸟名，古人早已细细辨认这类夜鸟；《诗经·墓门》借梅树上的鸮声起兴，接着唱出对人的劝诫，树影因而有了不安的意味。《豳风·鸱鸮》又写一只失子的鸟，趁天未雨修补巢穴，忧惧中仍忙着守家。夜色里的警觉、孤独与护巢之心，让这位服务员的圆眼睛，多了几层可以慢慢读出的情绪。

**灯亮才精神**

午后叫它三声，未必听见一声回答；夜里有人轻碰杯沿，它已经转过头来。鸮记桌位靠的是声音，最讨厌灌灌替它大声报号：“我听得见，隔壁街也听得见。”

**同一只鸟的两面**

有位远客说，故乡的猫头鹰常伴着雅典娜，意味着智慧。鸮听完，慢慢眨眼，把最后一盏灯拨亮。梅树、神殿与夜市沿着一声鸟鸣相逢，各自留下自己的夜色。

梅树与鸮声之后，诗句转向一场未被听见的劝告。

> 墓门有梅，有鸮萃止。夫也不良，歌以讯之。讯予不顾，颠倒思予。
> ——《诗经·陈风·墓门》
> By the Tomb Gate are plum-trees; Owls roost upon them.
Man, you are not good; I make this song to accuse you.
Accused you do not heed me; After your fall you will think of me.

> — The Book of Songs · Mumen

> English: Arthur Waley · The Book of Songs (1937)

墓门旁有梅树，鸮鸟聚在枝头。诗中人借歌劝诫那位行止不善的人；对方没有听取，等困顿时才会想起这些话。

另一首诗让鸟亲自开口，护住风雨前的巢室。

> 鸱鸮鸱鸮，既取我子，无毁我室。恩斯勤斯，鬻子之闵斯。迨天之未阴雨，彻彼桑土，绸缪牖户。
> ——《诗经·豳风·鸱鸮》
> Oh, kite-owl, kite-owl, You have taken my young. Do not destroy my house.
With such love, such toil To rear those young ones I strove!
Before the weather grew damp with rain I scratched away the bark of that mulberry-tree
And twined it into window and door.

> — The Book of Songs · Chixiao

> English: Arthur Waley · The Book of Songs (1937)

鸟儿呼告鸱鸮：既然已经夺走我的幼子，请留下我的巢。养育如此辛劳；趁风雨未至，还得取来桑根，缠补巢口。

**Xiao, the Night Owl**

Xiao works the night shift. Drowsy by daylight, this owl-eyed waiter wakes with the lanterns and hears an empty cup touch its saucer across the room.

**A voice by the plum tree**

The Book of Songs places owls in a plum tree beside a gateway. Chinese verse has heard solitude and darkness in their calls. Xiao arrives with those old associations: eyes at home where other creatures can barely see.

**A life in the old books**

Xiao's background reaches through poetry and the old classification of birds. The Erya lists chixiao among bird names. In the Book of Songs' Mumen, an owl in a plum tree introduces admonition and unease. In the poem Chixiao, a bird that has lost its young pleads for its nest and works before the rain arrives. Alertness, solitude and the effort to protect a home give the night waiter's eyes a richer emotional history than a single ominous call.

**Awake with the lamps**

Three afternoon summons may go unanswered. At night, one tap on a cup brings Xiao round immediately. It remembers tables by sound and grumbles when Guanguan shouts the numbers loudly enough for the next street.

**An owl abroad**

A traveller describes the owl beside Athena, a companion of wisdom. Xiao blinks and brightens the final lamp. A plum tree, a temple and a night market meet through the same bird, each retaining its own darkness and light.

### 耳鼠 · Ěr shǔ

耳鼠有松快的步子，也有一条能带它飞起来的尾巴。它负责端菜，最得意的本领是在满盘汤汁里保住最后一圈波纹。

**丹熏山的飞法**

丹熏山有树、有韭薤，水向西流；《北山经》还在那里安放了耳鼠。它形似鼠，带着兔首与大耳，偏偏“以其尾飞”。古人的笔让一条尾巴离开地面，也让这位小小异客有了自己的天空。

**典籍里的身世**

耳鼠住在丹熏山，山中樗柏、韭薤与熏水环绕。原文给它兔首、麋身，又说声音像嗥犬，最后才点出“以其尾飞”。鼠、兔、鹿与犬声被拼在一处，尾巴更接过了翅膀的差事。古人还把免于腹胀、抵御毒害的愿望寄在它身上。这样一位异兽的特色，既在飞得奇，也在身体里装着好几种熟悉生命的影子；每认出一处，丹熏山就又向读者打开一点。

**借过，借过**

它走快路时信心十足，见到突然挪开的凳子又会猛地停住。托盘比自己的胆子大，耳鼠对此颇有怨言。谁若肯先替它收一下椅脚，下一杯热茶准会被悄悄添满。

**把尾巴念给你听**

远来的孩子指着它问，翅膀藏在哪里。耳鼠索性绕空托盘飞一圈，再落回桌边。一个动作讲完了古书最奇的一句话；名字里的“耳”与“鼠”，也随笑声变得好记。

耳鼠的飞法，与它的头、身体和声音一起被记下。

> 有獸焉，其狀如䑕而兔首麋身，其音如獋犬，以其尾飛，名曰耳䑕，食之不䐆，又可以禦百毒。
> ——《山海经·北山经》·丹熏之山
> There is an animal shaped like a mouse, with a hare's head and a large deer's body. Its voice is like a barking hound, and it flies by means of its tail. It is called Ershu. Eating it prevents bloating and can ward off every poison.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Northern Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

这种兽带着鼠的形貌、兔的头和麋的身体，声音像嗥叫的犬，借尾巴飞翔，名为耳鼠。古书又把消除腹胀、抵御毒害的愿望写在它身上。

**Ershu, the Flying-Tailed Mouse**

Ershu carries dishes with quick feet and a flying tail. Its proudest achievement is delivering soup with the final ring of ripples still inside the bowl.

**A different way to fly**

On Danxun Mountain, the Northern Mountains describes a mouse-like creature with a rabbit's head and large ears. It flies by its tail. Familiar animals assemble into an unfamiliar life, with a patch of sky all its own.

**A life in the old books**

Ershu lives amid Danxun's trees, herbs and flowing water. The text assembles a rabbit's head, a deer's body and a hound-like voice before giving the tail its power of flight. Mouse, rabbit, deer and dog contribute familiar details to an unfamiliar whole. Old hopes of relief from bloating and protection from poisons accompany the description. Following these features lets a reader enter the mountain through several recognisable creatures, then discover the extraordinary way they have been joined.

**Coming through**

Ershu trusts its speed until somebody moves a stool. It insists its tray is larger than its courage. Clear a chair leg before it passes and an extra pour of tea may quietly appear at your table.

**A story in motion**

A visiting child asks where the wings are. Ershu circles an empty tray and lands. One movement explains the old text; er, ear, and shu, mouse, become words the child can remember.

### 灌灌 · Guàn guàn

灌灌负责招徕，嗓门比门前的铜铃还亮。它认定好菜值得整条街知道，连“今日售罄”也能喊出开张的气势。

**青丘先闻声**

青丘山除了九尾狐，还有形似鸠的灌灌。《南山经》说它的声音像人在呵呼，名字又像把鸣叫记了下来。山中有客未见其影，先闻其声；到了街头，它依旧是这一套出场办法。

**典籍里的身世**

灌灌与九尾狐同出青丘山，前者先以叫声使人记住，后者先以九尾引人回望。“其音若呵”让灌灌的声音近于人在呵呼，郭璞也循着这一点解释它；鸟名则像把那声呼唤又重复两遍。篇末的“佩之不惑”还赋予它使人免于迷惑的意味。青丘于是既有会迷住目光的尾影，也有仿佛能把人唤醒的声音，山林里的两位邻居，在小馆里仍然各自占着一份热闹。

**招牌要响亮**

它写招牌喜欢用大字，报菜名喜欢一口气，遇见梼杌还要争到底哪句更上口。若客人捂住耳朵，它会压低声音认真重说一遍，结果连悄声都透着一股开市的喜气。

**念一声灌灌**

Guàn guàn，两个音节落得短而亮。它教远客念名字，总先用筷子敲两下碗沿，让声调有了落脚处。中文里自呼其名的异鸟，就这样在另一种口音里又叫了一回。

古书写完九尾狐，又让另一位青丘邻居鸣叫出场。

> 有鳥焉，其狀如鳩，其音若呵，名曰灌灌，佩之不惑。
> ——《山海经·南山经》·青丘之山
> There is a bird shaped like a dove, whose voice sounds like a shout. It is called Guanguan. Wearing it keeps one from being bewildered.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Southern Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

青丘有鸟形似鸠，声音像人的呵呼，名叫灌灌。古人还寄望佩带它能使人不受迷惑。

**Guanguan, the Calling Bird**

Guanguan attracts guests with a voice brighter than the doorbell. It believes every good dish deserves an announcement loud enough for the whole street.

**Heard before seen**

Qingqiu has another neighbour besides the nine-tailed fox: a dove-like bird whose call resembles a human shout. The Southern Mountains names it Guanguan. Voice and name seem to answer each other across the hillside.

**A life in the old books**

Guanguan shares Qingqiu with the nine-tailed fox. One neighbour catches the eye; the other announces itself to the ear. Its call resembles a human shout, an association Guo Pu also follows, while its repeated name seems to echo that sound. The passage adds a hope of freedom from bewilderment when it is worn as a charm. Qingqiu thus holds both an entrancing silhouette and a voice that might call someone back to awareness, two contrasting presences now meeting in the inn.

**Loud lettering**

Guanguan favours enormous signs and menu calls delivered in one breath. It argues with Taowu over the catchiest wording. When a guest covers their ears, it earnestly whispers the announcement again, somehow still sounding like opening day.

**Say it twice**

Guàn guàn: two bright, falling syllables. Guanguan taps a bowl twice while teaching travellers its name. An old bird's self-announcing call finds another home, this time inside somebody else's accent.

### 讹兽 · É shòu

讹兽有兔一样的身形、人一样的面孔，还有一张能把凉茶说热的嘴。它替小馆讲故事，掌柜替它记住客人真正点了什么。

**一张颠倒话的嘴**

《神异经》里的讹兽能言，偏爱把东西南北、真假好坏倒着说。旅人若只追着声音走，恐怕要在它的故事里多绕几座山。那张近人的面孔，也因此添上了格外狡黠的神气。

**典籍里的身世**

讹兽来自《神异经》的西南荒中，兔形、人面，偏又善言。它说东却指西，说坏却道好，真假在嘴边轻轻一转，听者便容易跟着走错方向。故事还把这种颠倒传给吃过它肉的人，使谎言像一种会沾染的气味。“讹”本就带着错乱与欺诈的意思，名字、言语与遭遇于是扣成一个小小圈套。再听门前这位说书人铺陈梅香，便更能领会那份机巧里藏着的狡黠。

**菜单之外还有一篇**

它介绍酸梅汤，能从一缕梅香讲到月亮落山，偏偏记不住客人说的“少甜”。獬豸来核账时，它总先笑一笑：“这句我可没夸张。”桌上随即多了一张写得端正的小纸条。

**换一种语言的包袱**

有位远客听不懂双关，讹兽便画出一只酸得皱脸的梅子。笑声终于落下，它得意得耳朵都竖了起来。一个好故事有时靠口才，有时靠愿意重新讲一遍的耐心。

这一段把它从何处来、怎样说话，以及谎言如何传递，一气写完。

> 西南荒中出讹兽，其状若菟，人面能言。常欺人，言东而西，言恶而善。其肉美，食之言不真矣。
> ——《神异经》·讹兽条
> The southwestern wilderness produces Eshou, shaped like a hare, with a human face and the power of speech. It is always deceiving people: saying east when it means west, and bad when it means good. Its flesh is delicious, but those who eat it cease to speak the truth.

> — The Classic of Divine Marvels · Eshou

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

西南荒野有讹兽，兔形人面，能够说话。它常以颠倒的话欺人；故事甚至说，吃过它肉的人，也会跟着说出不真的话。

**Eshou, the Charming Storyteller**

Eshou has a rabbit's body, a human face and a tongue that could talk cold tea into boiling. It tells the inn's stories; the proprietor remembers the actual orders.

**A mouthful of reversals**

In the Classic of Divine Marvels, Eshou speaks and turns directions and meanings upside down. A traveller following its words might circle several mountains. Its almost-human face wears the additional mystery of a practised trickster.

**A life in the old books**

Eshou comes from the southwestern wilderness of the Classic of Divine Marvels. Rabbit-shaped and human-faced, it speaks fluently while reversing directions and judgments. The tale extends the reversal to anyone who eats its flesh, as though falsehood could travel from mouth to mouth. E, its name's first character, carries error and deception. Appearance, speech and consequence form a tightly constructed trickster story, giving the inn's eloquent promoter a history behind its charm and verbal mischief.

**Beyond the menu**

Eshou can describe plum fragrance until moonset and still forget a request for less sugar. When Xiezhi checks the order, it smiles: that part, surely, was no exaggeration. A carefully written note soon joins the table.

**Carrying a joke across**

A visitor misses its pun. Eshou draws a plum with a spectacularly sour face. Laughter arrives at last, and its ears rise proudly. The storyteller has discovered another route between languages.

### 饕餮 · Tāo tiè

饕餮掌勺，也试味，尝过一遍还想再尝。它最怕有人用“已经很好吃”搪塞自己，一双眼睛总追着锅里尚未成形的香气。

**鼎上的目光**

古代青铜礼器常饰醒目的兽面，后来人们惯称饕餮纹。《吕氏春秋》又写鼎上饕餮有首无身，吞食的欲望把它留成一张巨口。宴饮、威仪与食欲，在这张脸上相互凝望。

**典籍里的身世**

饕餮的身世在几种文本中越积越厚。《左传·文公十八年》把它写成缙云氏不才子，贪食聚财而不知满足；《吕氏春秋·先识》又借鼎上有首无身的形象讲贪欲自伤。《山海经·北山经》另有羊身人面、目在腋下的狍鸮，郭璞注把它联系到饕餮。人伦的恶名、鼎上的兽面与山中的异兽，就这样渐渐叠成今日熟悉的大胃口。那张巨口因而有历史的重量，也有被重新讲述的余地。

**再试一口**

它的味碟总比别人的多，评语也来得直：“香到了，鲜还差一点。”若新菜终于合意，它会站在灶前笑得肩膀发抖。伙计们得赶紧盛盘，否则这位主厨又要以试味为名伸筷子。

**从兽面到餐桌**

“老饕”在中文里也能亲切地称呼懂吃的人。一位客人学会这个词，便拿来夸饕餮；它听得高兴，终于把最后一份留给全桌分尝。古鼎上的大口，在人声里添了一点烟火气。

鼎上巨口的故事，把吞食与自伤连在了一起。

> 周鼎着饕餮，有首无身，食人未咽，害及其身，以言报更也。为不善亦然。
> ——《吕氏春秋·先识》
> The Zhou tripods bear the image of Taotie: a head without a body. It devours a person, but before swallowing, brings harm upon itself. This speaks of the return of consequences. So it is, too, with doing evil.

> — The Lüshi Chunqiu · Xianshi

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

周鼎饰着饕餮，有头无身，吞人还没有咽下，祸害已经落回自己身上。文章用它说明恶行会招来回报。

郭璞后来借这位狍鸮，解释饕餮的异兽形象。

> 有獸焉，其狀如羊身人面，其目在腋下，虎齒人爪，其音如嬰兒，名曰狍鴞，是食人。
> ——《山海经·北山经》·钩吾之山
> There is an animal with a sheep's body and a human face. Its eyes are beneath its arms; it has tiger's teeth and human claws. Its voice is like an infant's. It is called Paoxiao, and it eats people.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Northern Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

钩吾山有兽，羊身人面，眼在腋下，虎齿人爪，声音像婴儿，名叫狍鸮，以人为食。

另一条来路从人的贪欲写起。

> 缙云氏有不才子，贪于饮食，冒于货贿，侵欲崇侈，不可盈厌，聚敛积实，不知纪极，不分孤寡，不恤穷匮，天下之民以比三凶，谓之饕餮。
> ——《左传·文公十八年》
> The Jinyun clan had an unworthy son, greedy for food and drink and grasping after wealth. Covetous and extravagant, he could never be satisfied. He amassed goods without knowing any limit, shared nothing with orphans or widows, and showed no concern for the destitute. The people of the world ranked him with the other three calamities and called him Taotie.

> — The Zuo Tradition · Duke Wen, Year 18

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

缙云氏有不肖的后人，贪求饮食财货，奢侈无度，聚敛没有止境，又不顾孤寡穷困。天下人把他与三凶并列，称为饕餮。

**Taotie, the Great Appetite**

Taotie cooks and tastes, then tastes again. Praise alone never satisfies this chef: its eyes follow the fragrance of a dish still finding its shape.

**A face on bronze**

Powerful animal masks decorate ancient Chinese ritual bronzes and are commonly called taotie designs. The Lüshi Chunqiu describes a devouring creature, all head and no body. Appetite and ceremony look outward through the same immense mouth.

**A life in the old books**

Taotie's history accumulates across texts. The Zuo Tradition describes a descendant of Jinyun whose greed for food and possessions has no limit. The Lüshi Chunqiu gives a warning through a vessel's devouring, bodiless head. The Northern Mountains describes Paoxiao, sheep-bodied and human-faced, with eyes beneath its arms; Guo Pu links that creature with Taotie. A moral name, a bronze image and a mountain beast gradually converge. Their layered history gives the inn's great appetite more than one story to carry.

**One more taste**

Taotie keeps a crowd of tasting dishes. Fragrance right, savour still missing, it declares. When an experiment finally succeeds, its shoulders shake with delight. The others plate quickly before another supposedly essential sample disappears.

**An affectionate appetite**

Chinese also calls a devoted food lover a lao tao. A traveller learns the expression and tries it on the chef. Delighted, Taotie leaves the final helping for everyone to share. The bronze face has acquired a supper-table voice.

### 冉遗鱼 · Rǎn yí yú

冉遗鱼是照看汤锅的厨师，鱼身、蛇首，六只脚各有各的忙法。它说话缓慢，端碗却稳得连汤面上的葱花也懒得转身。

**白山与涴水**

英鞮山的鸟兽皆白，涴水从山中流出，冉遗之鱼游在其间。《西山经》给了它蛇首、鱼身与六足，也写下古人借它安眠的愿望。奇异的水中住客，背后藏着长夜得以安歇的盼头。

**典籍里的身世**

冉遗之鱼的故乡很有画面：英鞮山上多漆木，山下多金玉，鸟兽尽白，涴水向北流入陵羊之泽。它有鱼身、蛇首、六足，连眼睛都被比作马耳，处处出人意料。原文的“不眯”通常被释作免于梦魇，又与御凶并记。白色山林、流动水泽和安眠的愿望，把它围成一种清冷而安静的气息；汤灶旁的缓慢语声，仿佛还沿着那条水路传来。

**水声要听完**

它熬汤时嫌人说话太急，总要等滚水从响亮转为细密，才肯添下一味。别人催菜，它便用三只脚端碗、三只脚护炉，慢条斯理地答：“急的已经替你盛好了。”

**把故乡说慢一点**

有人把家乡清汤的名字写给它，有人只能比画一条鱼。冉遗鱼都接过来，慢慢念，仿佛名字也是一味需要温着的食材。碗沿腾起的水汽，让不同口音暂时变得一样轻。

山色、水路与异鱼的完整形貌，都在这一段里。

> 又西三百五十里曰英鞮之山，上多漆木，下多金玉，鳥獸盡白。涴水出焉，而北注于陵羊之澤，是多冉遺之魚，魚身蛇首六足，其目如馬耳，食之使人不眯，可以禦凶。
> ——《山海经·西山经》·英鞮之山
> Another three hundred and fifty li west is Mount Yingti. Lacquer trees abound above, metals and jade below, and all its birds and beasts are white. The Wan River rises there and flows north into Lingyang Marsh. Many Ranyi fish live in it: fish-bodied, serpent-headed, with six feet and eyes like horses' ears. Eating them keeps one free from nightmares and wards off harm.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Western Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

英鞮山上多漆树，山下有金玉，鸟兽都是白色。涴水向北流入陵羊泽，其中多冉遗鱼，鱼身蛇首六足，眼形似马耳。古书把安眠避凶的愿望也寄在它身上。

**Ranyiyu, the Six-Legged Fish**

Ranyiyu tends the soup pots: fish-bodied, serpent-headed and furnished with six busy feet. It speaks slowly and carries bowls so steadily that the garnish barely turns.

**White mountain, flowing water**

The Western Mountains places this strange fish in the Wanshui, below a mountain of white birds and beasts. Alongside its remarkable anatomy, the old account preserves a hope of peaceful sleep. A creature of water carries a very human wish through the night.

**A life in the old books**

Ranyiyu belongs to a vividly described landscape: lacquer trees above Yingtishan, metals and jade below, white birds and beasts, and Wanshui running north to a marsh. Its fish body, serpent head and six feet are joined by eyes compared to a horse's ears. The phrase bu mi is commonly understood as freedom from nightmares, alongside protection from misfortune. White woodland, moving water and hopes for peaceful sleep give the creature an atmosphere that still seems to linger beside its quiet soup stove.

**Listen to the simmer**

Ranyiyu waits for loud boiling to settle before adding another ingredient. Urgent orders receive a leisurely reply: those bowls are already ready. Three feet carry them while the other three mind the stove.

**Names kept warm**

Guests write the names of soups from home or sketch a fish. Ranyiyu sounds them out as carefully as it seasons broth. Different accents become lighter together above the steaming bowls.

### 当康 · Dāng kāng

当康是报丰收的山海来客，在店里做服务员。它喜欢看米缸满起来，添饭时总多抖半勺，问就是：“这一勺自己滑下去的。”

**獠牙间的好消息**

《东山经》的当康形似有牙的猪，叫声像呼唤自己的名字，现身便预示天下“大穰”。两颗獠牙托着的，竟是谷物满仓的喜讯。年成的好坏，曾牵动无数人家灶上的炊烟。

**典籍里的身世**

当康住在《东山经》的钦山，那里多金玉，师水从山间流出，水中又有鱼与文贝。古书给它的形貌很朴素：像猪，长牙；叫声却像在喊自己的名字。最重的一笔落在“大穰”，把这只山兽与天下丰收相连。古人记异兽，常把会带来旱涝的存在与报年成的瑞兆并写；当康恰好站在盼望的那一边，一声自呼其名，就使谷仓与饭桌都仿佛近了些。

**米缸旁的心事**

它热衷盘点新米，却不爱算有几人添了饭。掌柜一问，它就把大耳朵往米袋上一搭。等厨房忙完，当康一定再问一句谁还没吃；这时候，藏起来的那半勺才有了去处。

**丰年的声音**

秋收家宴、收获节的长桌，各有自己的祝词。远客问它怎样念“大穰”，当康先指米，再指满堂碗筷。人们还没把那个古字学会，已经听懂了这份热热闹闹的好年景。

形貌、名字的声音和丰年的预兆，在这里连成一句完整的介绍。

> 有獸焉，其狀如豚而有牙，其名曰當康，其鳴自叫，見則天下大穰。
> ——《山海经·东山经》·钦山
> There is an animal shaped like a pig, but with tusks. Its name is Dangkang, and its cry calls out that name. When it appears, the whole world enjoys a great harvest.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Eastern Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

有兽形似猪而长着獠牙，名叫当康，叫声像呼唤自己的名字；它出现时，预示天下丰收。

**Dangkang, Herald of the Harvest**

Dangkang announces a good harvest and serves the inn's tables. It loves a full rice bin and an overly generous scoop: that extra half, it insists, slipped in by itself.

**Good news between tusks**

The Eastern Mountains describes a tusked, pig-like creature calling its own name. Its arrival foretells da rang, an abundant harvest. Behind those two tusks lies the promise of grain for the households whose cooking fires depend on the season.

**A life in the old books**

Dangkang lives on Qin Mountain in the Eastern Mountains, amid metals, jade, river fish and patterned shells. Its anatomy is simple: pig-like, with tusks. The memorable turn lies in a call resembling its own name and the promise of da rang, abundant harvest. Around such creatures, the Classic also records drought and flood omens. Dangkang stands on the hopeful side of that uncertain world, drawing fields, grain stores and the dinner table closer with one recognisable call.

**Beside the rice bin**

Dangkang counts new sacks eagerly and second helpings reluctantly. Asked about portions, it drapes an ear over the ledger. Once service ends, however, it checks every worker has eaten. The missing half-scoops have found their destination.

**The sound of plenty**

Guests describe harvest feasts and autumn tables. Asked to explain da rang, Dangkang points first at rice, then at the filled bowls. The unfamiliar old word already has a welcoming sound.

### 天狗 · Tiān gǒu

天狗照看门前招牌，也守收银台。白头在灯下一晃，谁多付了一枚钱、谁落下一把伞，它都记得清清楚楚。

**阴山的白首**

《西山经》说阴山有兽，形似狸，头部白色，名为天狗，能御凶。后来的民间故事又让天狗追上月亮，把月食讲成一场吞食。山中的白首与天上的巨口，替同一个名字照出了两幅影子。

**典籍里的身世**

阴山的天狗伴着浊浴之水出场，白首狸形，声音被记作“榴榴”，还承担御凶的愿望。《史记·天官书》的“天狗”则进入星占：像大流星，有声，落向地面时又被比作狗。再往后，民间把吞月的巨口也交给这个名字。一只山兽、一种天象、一段月食故事，沿着同名的线各自延伸。它们让小馆门口那位白首伙伴，多出了一片可以抬头追问的夜空。

**门要留一线**

它数钱时不许插话，听到门响却总先抬头。最后一位客人离开，它还要望一眼街口，才把招牌翻过来。有时掌柜催它收工，天狗便指着雨棚：“伞的主人还没回来。”

**同名的远客**

有人从日本天狗的长鼻与鸟翼说起，天狗便侧过白头，让对方看看自己的模样。两种形象沿着同一组汉字碰面，像旅人交换各自画过的月亮，越看越有话可说。

山中的天狗有自己的形貌和声音。

> 有獸焉，其狀如狸而白首，名曰天狗，其音如榴榴，可以禦凶。
> ——《山海经·西山经》·阴山
> There is an animal shaped like a wildcat, with a white head. It is called Tiangou, and its voice goes 'liu-liu'. It can ward off harm.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Western Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

阴山有兽，形似狸而头白，名叫天狗，叫声被写作“榴榴”。古人也赋予它抵御凶险的力量。

同一个名字来到天上，被用来描述奇异的星象。

> 天狗，状如大奔星，有声，其下止地，类狗。
> ——《史记·天官书》
> Tiangou resembles a great rushing star and makes a sound. Where it comes to rest upon the ground, it resembles a dog.

> — Records of the Grand Historian · Celestial Offices

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

星占中的天狗像一颗巨大的奔落之星，伴着声响，落向地面时被形容为近于狗的形态。

**Tiangou, the White-Headed Guardian**

Tiangou watches the sign and the till. Beneath its white head, a sharp memory keeps track of an extra coin, a missing umbrella and every opening of the door.

**Two shadows of a name**

The Western Mountains describes a white-headed, wildcat-like Tiangou warding off danger. Later Chinese tales send a heavenly dog after the moon. A mountain creature and an eclipse cast strikingly different shadows under one name.

**A life in the old books**

The Tiangou of Yin Mountain appears beside the Zhuoyu River: white-headed, cat-like, with a call written liuliu and a role in warding off danger. In the Records of the Grand Historian, the name enters celestial divination as a sounding, meteor-like apparition. Later moon-swallowing stories give it another outline. Mountain creature, sky phenomenon and eclipse tale extend through one name. They place a much wider night sky behind the white-headed companion standing at the inn's door.

**A door left ajar**

Tiangou dislikes interruptions while counting, yet looks up at every footstep. Long after closing, it waits beneath the awning for an umbrella's owner. The cash is settled; apparently the evening is not.

**A namesake arrives**

A guest describes Japanese tengu, their long noses and wings. Tiangou turns its white head for inspection. Shared written characters carry the conversation between distinct images, like travellers comparing the moons they have drawn.

### 蛊雕 · Gǔ diāo

蛊雕似雕而有角，开口却像婴儿啼哭。它在小馆做服务员，不善寒暄，摆碗时一只只排得笔直，连筷尖都朝着同一边。

**鹿吴山的回声**

《南山经》把蛊雕留在多金石的鹿吴山，记它有角、善发婴儿般的声音，又写它食人。那一声啼哭在古书里带着险意，像山林深处忽然亮起一盏令人犹疑的灯。

**典籍里的身世**

蛊雕所居的鹿吴山不长草木，却多金石；泽更之水从这里流出，而古书紧接着在水中写出这位有角的异兽。它的形似雕，声音却是婴儿的啼哭，食人的记载又使这份柔弱声音忽然变得危险。山林、水泽、猛禽与婴儿声互相错置，构成蛊雕最难忘的魅力。读全这一段，便能听出它为何总让旅人先停步，再回头寻找声音真正的来处。

**今日先点头**

它初来时一声招呼惊动满堂，从此学会先点头，再递菜单。可谁若把餐盘胡乱堆在桌沿，它的角便绷得更直。忙完回头一看，险些摔落的碗早已安稳落在它手里。

**把陌生留给名字**

一位远客在纸上画出家乡传说里的怪鸟，蛊雕低头研究许久，补上自己的一对角。两幅画并排压在茶杯下面。它还没想好怎样开口，对方已经认出了这份郑重的回应。

古书先写山与水，再让叫声古怪的蛊雕出场。

> 又東五百里曰鹿吳之山，上無草木，多金石。澤更之水出焉，而南流注于滂水。水有獸焉，名曰蠱雕，其狀如雕而有角，其音如嬰兒之音，是食人。
> ——《山海经·南山经》·鹿吴之山
> Another five hundred li east is Mount Luwu. No grass or trees grow on it, but metals and stone abound. The Zegeng River rises there and flows south into the Pang River. An animal lives in the water, called Gudiao. It resembles an eagle, but has horns. Its voice sounds like an infant's, and it eats people.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Southern Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

鹿吴山无草木而多金石，泽更水由此南流入滂水。水中有蛊雕，形似雕而有角，声音像婴儿，并有食人的习性。

**Gudiao, the Antlered Bird**

Gujiao has a bird of prey's form, horns and an infant-like cry. This reserved waiter lines bowls up precisely, with every chopstick tip pointing the same way.

**An unsettling mountain call**

The Southern Mountains places Gujiao on Luwu Mountain and describes its horns, strange voice and appetite for humans. An infant's cry carries danger through the old landscape, drawing a traveller's ear toward something unfamiliar.

**A life in the old books**

Gujiao's home is Luwu Mountain, bare of vegetation but rich in metals and stone. The Zegeng River emerges there, and the text locates the horned creature in its waters. A bird-of-prey's form meets an infant's cry, while the account of man-eating turns that vulnerable sound into danger. Mountain, water, raptor and crying child overlap in one unsettling image. The fuller passage explains why a traveller might stop, listen and only then search for the source of the voice.

**A nod before speaking**

One greeting once startled the whole dining room. Gujiao now offers a nod and a menu first. Its horns stiffen at a carelessly stacked plate; a moment later, the endangered bowl rests safely in its hands.

**A drawing answers**

A visitor sketches a legendary bird from home. Gujiao studies it and adds its own horns on another sheet. Two pictures lie beneath their teacups. Before the waiter finds the right words, its careful reply has already been understood.

### 旋龟 · Xuán Guī

旋龟有龟身、鸟首与蛇尾，是小馆里最稳当的厨师。它动作慢，记性却细，别人的锅少了一瓢水，它往往先于主人发现。

**怪水的木声**

《南山经》写杻阳山的怪水里有旋龟，形似龟而鸟首虺尾，声音如判木。厚重龟壳里藏着清脆的回响，古人也把避疾求安的愿望寄在它身上。熟悉的轮廓，由此长出了几处奇峰。

**典籍里的身世**

旋龟来自杻阳山流出的怪水，顺水可到宪翼之水。它龟形、鸟首、虺尾，声音却像木头被劈开，厚重身体与清脆声响形成奇妙反差。古书又记“佩之不聋”，郭璞对篇末“为”字作“治”解，让这一段还寄着护听与康复的愿望。它的特色远不止稳重长寿：水路、异形、木声和古人的日用心愿共同围着那副龟壳，读到末尾，仿佛还能听见山中轻轻一响。

**慢半拍的准头**

开火以前，它先把调料排好；关火以后，还要把台面擦净。穷奇常嫌它慢，回头却又来借它记下的火候。旋龟从不追问，只用蛇尾点一点案边早已摆好的小纸片。

**时间盛在壳里**

许多地方的龟故事都带着时间的分量，旋龟却还多着鸟首与木响般的声音。来客听它说话，常先愣一愣，再笑着放慢语速。一顿饭的工夫，竟也能学会另一种从容。

旋龟的形貌、名字、声音与古人赋予的用途，接在同一段里。

> 其狀如龜而鳥首虺尾，其名曰旋龜，其音如判木，佩之不聾，可以為底。
> ——《山海经·南山经》·杻阳之山
> It is shaped like a turtle, with a bird's head and a small serpent's tail. Its name is Xuangui, and its voice sounds like wood being split. Wearing it prevents deafness; it can also be used against bodily ailments.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Southern Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

它像龟，却有鸟首蛇尾，名旋龟，声音像劈木。篇末又记“佩之不聋”，古注以“治”释“为”，将它与护听、祛病的愿望相连。

**Xuangui, the Steady River Turtle**

Xuangui is turtle-bodied, bird-headed and serpent-tailed. Slow at the stove but exact in memory, it often notices another cook's pot needs water before they do.

**A wooden sound from the water**

The Southern Mountains gives Xuangui a call like splitting wood. Its familiar shell contains unexpected anatomy and a clear, startling voice. The old account also carries a wish for relief from illness, resting within this unusual water creature.

**A life in the old books**

Xuangui comes from the Guaishui flowing out of Niuyang Mountain. Turtle shape, bird head and serpent tail are joined by a call like splitting wood, a striking sound from such a heavy outline. The text associates wearing it with protection from deafness; Guo Pu reads the passage's final wording in relation to healing. Waterway, anatomy, voice and hopes of recovery surround the shell. Its identity contains much more than patience or longevity; the old description leaves a clear wooden resonance behind.

**Half a beat slower**

Before cooking, Xuangui arranges the seasonings. Afterwards, it cleans. Qiongqi complains about the pace and later borrows its timing notes. A serpent's tail quietly points toward the slip already waiting on the counter.

**Time in a shell**

Turtle stories in many places carry the weight of time. This one adds a bird's head and a wooden resonance. Guests pause at its voice, smile and slow their own speech, finding another tempo within a meal.

### 青蚨 · Qīng Fú

青蚨是灵巧的服务员，名字也曾被借来称钱。它眼尖，拾起一枚散落的零钱很快，记起那枚钱应当还给谁，却比什么都认真。

**母虫的归路**

《搜神记》讲青蚨形似蝉，母虫总会飞向被带走的幼子。相寻的力量又进入铜钱的故事，于是有了青蚨还钱的想象。后来诗文以青蚨代钱，冷硬金属的名字里便藏进了一段牵挂。

**典籍里的身世**

《搜神记》把青蚨写作南方一种似蝉而稍大的虫，产子依附草叶，母虫无论远近都能寻到被取走的幼子。故事继而把相寻的力量借给钱币，母钱、子钱花出以后又会飞回，循环无已，便有了“青蚨还钱”。从草叶上的小卵到铜钱的来去，牵挂与财富被一条想象的归路连接。后来“青蚨”成为钱的别称，读懂这个名字，便也读懂古人怎样让日常财货带上一点有生命的情意。

**哪一枚是你的**

它把失物分开放好，偏偏有人觉得零钱混在一起无妨。青蚨听完，立即翻出包得整齐的小纸包：这是饭钱，那是寄存，还有一枚是孩子攒来买饮料的，谁也替代不了谁。

**钱也会借一个名字**

贝壳、金属、颜色，都曾在不同语言里成为钱的别称。中文把一只小虫也收进其中。远客听完那条母子的归路，再把零钱放回口袋，指尖仿佛也多了一点温热的声响。

先读母虫寻子的本事，再看它怎样走进还钱的传说。

> 生子必依草叶，大如蚕子，取其子，母即飞来，不以远近，虽潜取其子，母必知处。
> ——《搜神记》·青蚨条
> It always lays its young upon blades of grass, as large as silkworm eggs. Take the young away, and the mother will fly to them, however far or near. Even if they are taken in secret, she will know where they are.

> — Records of the Supernatural · Qingfu

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

青蚨把卵产在草叶上，大小如蚕卵。幼子被取走后，母虫不论远近都会飞来，即使暗中取走，也知道幼子在哪里。

母子相寻的力量，又被故事借到钱币身上。

> 或先用母钱，或先用子钱，皆复飞归。轮转无已。故淮南子术以之还钱，名曰“青蚨。”
> ——《搜神记》·青蚨还钱
> Whether the mother coins or the child coins are spent first, they all fly back again. The circulation never ends. Thus the arts of the Huainan masters use it to bring money back, and call it 'Qingfu'.

> — Records of the Supernatural · Qingfu

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

传说中的母钱、子钱，无论先花哪一份都会飞回，循环不尽，于是青蚨与还钱的法术联系起来。

**Qingfu, the Returning Coin**

Qingfu is a nimble waiter whose name also became a Chinese word for money. It spots a fallen coin quickly and takes even greater care remembering its owner.

**A mother's return**

In the Records of the Supernatural, a cicada-like qingfu mother flies to her separated young. Stories lend that returning power to coins. When poetry later borrows the insect's name for money, affection travels inside a word for metal.

**A life in the old books**

The Records of the Supernatural describes a southern insect, cicada-like but larger, laying its young on leaves. The mother can find them however far they are taken. The story lends this returning power to coins: mother and child money fly back after being spent, circulating endlessly. A route of affection connects eggs on a leaf to the movement of currency. When qingfu later becomes a word for money, an everyday possession retains a trace of living attachment within its name.

**Which coin is yours?**

Qingfu keeps lost items separately. Suggest mixing the coins and neat paper packets appear: supper money, a deposit, a child's savings for a drink. Each sum has its own story and destination.

**Money borrowing names**

Shells, metals and colours have named money in different languages. Chinese adds this small insect. After hearing its story, a visitor pockets their change with a new association: a familiar sound now carries the idea of finding a way home.

### 九尾狐 · Jiǔ wěi hú

九尾狐从青丘来，九条尾巴各有各的心情。它是善于察言观色的服务员，笑起来让人放松，记性却好得叫熟客不敢随口许诺。

**九道尾影**

《南山经》的青丘之狐九尾、声如婴儿，带着危险而迷人的气息。后来，九尾白狐也走进祥瑞与婚姻的叙事。一片尾影能遮住山林，也能铺成好兆头，古人始终舍不得只给它一种面貌。

**典籍里的身世**

青丘九尾狐的原貌既奇丽也凶险：九尾、婴儿声，又能食人；同一段还给它附上免于蛊害的愿望。《吴越春秋·吴余外传》把九尾白狐写入大禹到涂山的故事，白色与九尾被解释为服色、王者之兆，并与婚娶相连。一只狐便从山中危险的住客，走到王者与婚姻的象征之中。后来东亚狐故事的万千面貌，都更值得从这些各有温度的旧文本，一页页认起。

**它记得上次**

谁爱窗边，谁的茶要淡，谁说下回请全桌喝一杯，九尾狐都记得。它提醒人时从不提高嗓门，只把最后一条尾巴慢慢卷起来。客人便笑着投降，唤它把茶添满。

**尾巴越过海**

日本的狐、韩国的九尾狐与中国青丘故事，在东亚的书页和屏幕间留下许多回声。远客各讲一种，九尾狐便一条条数自己的尾巴，仿佛九条刚好够装下今晚还没说完的传闻。

青丘最早的一道尾影，同时带着奇貌与危险。

> 有獸焉，其狀如狐而九尾，其音如嬰兒，能食人，食者不蠱。
> ——《山海经·南山经》·青丘之山
> There is an animal shaped like a fox, but with nine tails. Its voice is like an infant's. It can eat people; those who eat it are free from gu enchantment.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Southern Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

青丘有兽形似狐而长九尾，叫声像婴儿，能够食人；古书又记食其肉可免蛊害的说法。

白狐在另一部书里走向婚娶与王者的吉兆。

> 乃有白狐九尾造于禹。禹曰：「白者，吾之服也。其九尾者，王之证也。」
> ——《吴越春秋·吴余外传》·禹至涂山
> Then a white fox with nine tails came to Yu. Yu said, 'White is the colour of my robes. Those nine tails are a sign of kingship.'

> — The Spring and Autumn Annals of Wu and Yue · Wuyu

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

一只九尾白狐来到禹面前。禹把白色看作自己的服色，把九尾看作王者的象征。故事随后接向涂山的歌与婚娶。

**Jiuweihu, the Nine-Tailed Fox**

Jiuweihu comes from Qingqiu, with nine tails and nine shifting moods. This perceptive waiter puts guests at ease and remembers every promise a regular makes.

**Nine shadows**

The Southern Mountains gives Qingqiu's fox nine tails, an infant-like voice and a dangerous allure. Other old narratives make the white nine-tailed fox auspicious. Its many tails carry several histories at once, from unsettling hillside encounters to wedding blessings.

**A life in the old books**

Qingqiu's fox is both marvellous and dangerous: nine tails, an infant's voice and a taste for humans, alongside an old promise of protection from harmful enchantment. In the Spring and Autumn Annals of Wu and Yue, a white nine-tailed fox appears in Yu's journey to Tushan; colour and tails become signs associated with kingship and marriage. The fox moves from wild encounter to auspicious interpretation. These distinct textual lives offer a richer starting point for the fox traditions that later travelled through East Asia.

**Remember last time?**

Jiuweihu remembers the window seat, the weaker tea and the guest who offered to buy everyone's next round. It makes its reminder quietly, curling the final tail. The guest laughs, surrenders and orders more tea.

**Tails across the sea**

Japanese fox tales, Korean nine-tailed foxes and Chinese Qingqiu stories echo across East Asian books and screens. Visitors offer their versions. Jiuweihu counts its tails as though allocating room for all the stories still to come.

### 毕方 · Bì fāng

毕方一足、青羽、赤纹、白喙，是灶边最醒目的厨师。它擅长烤制，脾气也像炭火：外面收着，里面一直亮。

**火色落在羽上**

《西山经》写毕方形似鹤而独足，现身与火灾的征兆相连。青与赤、静羽与烈焰，被古人并在一只鸟的身上。它站着不动时，仍像有一线火光从羽纹里缓缓经过。

**典籍里的身世**

章莪山无草木，多瑶碧，又有种种怪异，毕方便在这样的山色里显出青身赤纹。原文还写它白喙、一足，叫声像自呼其名；“见则其邑有讹火”则把鸟的出现与当地火灾相连。毕方的火意因此有一种警醒的锋芒，像灾变将至前先落进山林的征兆。等它站到小馆烤炉边，独足与赤纹都还认得出来，只是那份令人屏息的火光，如今让人守着一盘将熟的晚饭。

**翻面只有一次机会**

它烤东西不爱旁人指点，夹子落下之前，眼睛已经把火色看过几遍。饕餮若偷尝还没出炉的一块，必会挨一下白喙的冷眼。等炭火退到最合适的温度，它才肯说：“现在。”

**认火，也认风**

有客人谈起故乡会重生的火鸟，毕方只拨开炉灰，露出下面尚红的炭。每一团火都有自己的故事；它的这一团，教人记住风向、距离，以及焦香刚刚出现的那一瞬。

颜色、独足、自鸣与火兆，共同构成毕方的原貌。

> 有鳥焉，其狀如鶴，一足，赤文青質而白喙，名曰畢方，其鳴自叫也，見則其邑有譌火。
> ——《山海经·西山经》·章莪之山
> There is a bird shaped like a crane, with one leg, red markings on a blue-green body, and a white beak. It is called Bifang; its cry calls out its own name. When it appears, an uncanny fire occurs in that settlement.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Western Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

有鸟像鹤，只有一足，青身赤纹而白喙，名叫毕方，鸣声像呼唤自己的名字。它出现时，预示当地会有异常火灾。

**Bifang, the One-Legged Firebird**

Bifang is unmistakable beside the grill: one leg, blue-green feathers, red markings and a white beak. Its temper resembles charcoal, quiet outside and bright within.

**Fire on a feather**

The Western Mountains associates this crane-like, one-legged bird with the warning of fire. Blue and red, stillness and flame share one body. Even at rest, Bifang seems to carry an ember slowly travelling beneath its plumage.

**A life in the old books**

Bifang appears on Zhang'e Mountain, a bare landscape of fine stones and strange things. Blue-green body, red markings, white beak and a single leg create its vivid outline. Its call resembles its name; its appearance foretells fire in the settlement. Fire here has the sharpness of a warning arriving before disaster. Beside the inn's grill, the same leg and markings remain recognisable, while the tense attention once directed toward an omen now attends a plate almost ready for supper.

**The moment to turn**

It dislikes advice at the grill. Taotie earns a cold look for sampling anything unfinished. Bifang studies the coals, waits, then says now. The tongs move once; the crisp edge arrives exactly where it wanted.

**Reading wind and heat**

A guest describes a reborn firebird from home. Bifang brushes aside ash to reveal living red coals. Each fire has a story. This one teaches the distance of a hand, the direction of a draught and the instant fragrance begins.

### 貔貅 · Pí xiū

貔貅守着钱柜，算盘声能压住门外的雨。它喜欢进账，更喜欢账目合拢时那一下清脆的响，谁想糊弄一枚铜钱都不容易。

**猛兽走入钱袋**

《史记》的貔貅与熊罴等猛兽并列，带着早期传说的威势。后来，吉祥兽的形象进入摆件和佩饰，人们又把守护与聚财的愿望交给它。古老的利爪，渐渐握住了日常生活里的盼头。

**典籍里的身世**

《史记·五帝本纪》把貔貅列在轩辕修德振兵的宏大叙事里，与熊、罴、貙、虎并称，随后接向阪泉之战。这里留下的是威猛与征伐的声势，尚未细画它的角、翼与身形。貔貅后来进入吉祥兽与聚财的传统，形象与含义才不断增添。读它的来历，可以先听见古战场的一声兽名，再看那股力量怎样被请到门前、掌心与钱柜旁，替日常生活守住一份盼望。

**先把这笔说清**

它会为少收一文追到门口，也会把多付的钱原样送回。哪笔是饭钱，哪笔是预支，伙计们觉得差不多，它偏要分明。临近打烊，貔貅把算盘一推，终于肯为自己倒一杯凉茶。

**掌心的一只瑞兽**

客人拿出家乡的幸运小物，貔貅便把钱柜上的兽形转给对方看。金属、珠玉、磨旧的绳结，各自贴着一只掌心。那些想把明天过好的愿望，摸起来竟有相近的温度。

貔貅先以一声威猛的名字进入轩辕的征战叙事。

> 轩辕乃修德振兵，治五气，蓺五种，抚万民，度四方，教熊罴貔貅貙虎，以与炎帝战於阪泉之野。
> ——《史记·五帝本纪》·阪泉之战前
> Xuanyuan cultivated virtue and readied his troops, ordered the five vital influences, planted the five grains, cared for the people, and surveyed the four quarters. He trained bears, brown bears, pi and xiu beasts, chu beasts and tigers to fight the Flame Emperor on the plain of Banquan.

> — Records of the Grand Historian · Five Emperors

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

轩辕修明德政、整治军伍，理时气、兴种植、安抚万民，又教练熊、罴、貔貅、貙、虎，与炎帝战于阪泉之野。

**Pixiu, the Auspicious Guardian**

Pixiu keeps the till, its abacus audible above the rain. Income pleases it; a balanced account pleases it more. A single coin is difficult to explain away.

**From fierce beast to lucky charm**

The Records of the Grand Historian lists pixiu among formidable beasts. Later auspicious images entered ornaments and pendants, carrying wishes for protection and prosperity. The fierce creature acquired a place beside everyday hopes for a secure livelihood.

**A life in the old books**

In the Records of the Grand Historian, Pixiu stands among bears, chu and tigers within Xuanyuan's preparations for the battle of Banquan. The passage conveys martial force rather than a detailed description of horns, wings or body. Later auspicious and wealth-associated traditions add new meanings and forms. Its story can be followed from a powerful name in an epic account to an image invited beside doors, pendants and cash boxes, where fierceness gradually becomes a guardian of everyday hopes.

**Settle this entry first**

Pixiu follows a missing coin to the door and returns an overpayment just as promptly. Supper money and an advance require separate entries. Only when the accounts meet does it push the abacus aside and pour its own tea.

**A creature in the palm**

A guest produces a lucky object from home. Pixiu turns its little ornament toward them. Metal, jade and worn cord rest in their hands: different shapes, each warmed by somebody's hope for tomorrow.

### 英招 · Yīngsháo

英招有人面、马身、虎纹与鸟翼，是从园圃走向四海的神。它在小馆接待旅人，衣角仿佛还沾着各地草木的气味。

**槐江山的园丁**

《西山经》说英招照管天帝园圃，又巡行四海。虎纹与羽翼让它威仪不凡，园圃却把这份力量系在草木、雨水与时节上。它能飞向极远的地方，也记得一片新叶何时该舒展开来。

**典籍里的身世**

英招照看的地方是槐江山的“帝之平圃”，山中积着琅玕、金玉，远望昆仑又见光气。它马身人面、虎纹鸟翼，形貌把奔行与飞翔放在一处，职责却落在园圃的照管上；“狥于四海”又让它走出一隅，向四海巡行。守园与远游因此并在同一位神身上。它在小馆问起旅人故乡的树木时，那份好奇，仿佛仍带着槐江山远望四方的目光。

**桌边有风土**

它爱问客人的故乡有什么树，听到陌生菜名便找纸记下。忙时陆吾嫌它聊得太久，英招就把笔往耳后一别，先端菜，过会儿再从那棵没说完的树接着问。

**叶子夹在菜单里**

菜单里常夹着旅人送的小叶片，旁边写着几种语言的名字。有的香气与故乡相似，有的第一次闻见。英招把它们收在一起，翻页时，一张餐桌也能吹来许多个地方的风。

园圃、神名、形貌与巡行的职责，都在这一段相接。

> 實惟帝之平圃，神英招司之，其狀馬身而人面，虎文而鳥翼，狥于四海。
> ——《山海经·西山经》·槐江之山
> This is the celestial ruler's level garden, overseen by the god Yingzhao. It has a horse's body and a human face, tiger markings and bird wings, and travels around the Four Seas.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Western Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

这里是天帝的平圃，由英招照管。英招马身人面，有虎纹、鸟翼，又巡行于四海。

**Yingzhao, Keeper of the Celestial Garden**

Yingzhao has a human face, a horse's body, tiger markings and wings. A travelling garden-keeper now welcomes the inn's guests, bringing the scent of distant leaves.

**The garden on Huaijiang**

The Western Mountains makes Yingzhao guardian of the celestial gardens and a traveller across the seas. Its striking body belongs equally to flight and careful tending: a creature that can reach distant horizons and remember when a new leaf should open.

**A life in the old books**

Yingzhao tends the celestial ruler's level garden on Huaijiang, among precious stones and metals, with radiant Kunlun visible in the distance. Horse body, human face, tiger markings and bird wings combine running with flight. Its charge is the garden, yet the phrase xun yu si hai sends it around the seas. Tending one place and travelling far belong to the same god. The waiter's curiosity about a traveller's trees can still seem to carry Huaijiang's outward-looking gaze.

**A tree beside the table**

It asks visitors about trees at home and writes down unfamiliar dishes. Luwu complains the conversation takes too long. Yingzhao tucks its pencil behind an ear, delivers supper and returns to the unfinished tree.

**Leaves in the menu**

Small leaves offered by travellers rest between menu pages, labelled in several languages. Some fragrances recall home; others are entirely new. Turn a page and several different winds seem to cross the same table.

### 陆吾 · Lùwú

陆吾有人面、虎身与九尾，曾守昆仑帝苑，如今掌小馆的烤炉。它做事讲次序，连九条尾巴转身时都像各有差遣。

**昆仑有管事**

《西山经》称昆仑是天帝下都，陆吾掌管其间，又司天之九部与帝苑时节。神山的宏大落在一个“管”字里：风物有时，园囿有序，总得有谁把纷繁的事情一件件接住。

**典籍里的身世**

陆吾守的是昆仑之丘，古书称它为“帝之下都”，把神山写成天帝在人间的都城。它虎身九尾、人面虎爪，既有猛兽的威势，又带着神的职守；九部与园囿时节都在它的照管之下。昆仑周围还有沙棠、薲草与奇鸟异兽，像一座有生命、有物产、也有秩序的宏大园苑。认识陆吾，便能从那一张威严的脸，看见山海世界里如何把守护与治理想象成具体的生命。

**先出这一盘**

午市里锅勺齐响，它只抬一下虎爪，就把先后排妥。毕方不服它插手火候，陆吾也不退让：“你管那一块熟得好，我管这一桌吃得齐。”两人争完，热盘倒总能同时出门。

**山顶的另一种高度**

远客说起故乡神山上的宫殿，陆吾先问那里由谁照看。话题从云上的神明落到开门、扫阶、候时的人。昆仑因此有了可走近的一面：高处，也有人认真过着每一日。

昆仑的地位，与陆吾的身体和职守一同呈现。

> 西南四百里曰崑崙之丘，是實惟帝之下都，神陸吾司之。其神狀虎身而九尾，人面而虎爪，是神也司天之九部及帝之囿時。
> ——《山海经·西山经》·昆仑之丘
> Four hundred li to the southwest is the hill of Kunlun, the celestial ruler's lower capital. The god Luwu oversees it. This god has a tiger's body and nine tails, a human face and tiger's claws. It governs the nine divisions of heaven and the seasons of the ruler's gardens.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Western Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

昆仑是天帝的下都，由陆吾掌管。陆吾虎身九尾、人面虎爪，照管天上九部与天帝园苑的时节。

**Luwu, Steward of Kunlun**

Luwu once guarded Kunlun's divine gardens. Human-faced, tiger-bodied and nine-tailed, it now manages the grill with an impressive sense of order.

**Someone must keep Kunlun**

The Western Mountains calls Kunlun the celestial ruler's earthly capital. Luwu oversees its domains and the garden's seasons. A vast sacred landscape becomes imaginable through tending: somebody attends to each season, gate and changing task.

**A life in the old books**

Luwu guards Kunlun, described as the celestial ruler's earthly capital. Tiger body, nine tails, human face and tiger claws join animal force to divine office. The nine divisions of heaven and the garden's seasons fall within its care. Around it, Shatang trees, Pincao grass and extraordinary birds and beasts make Kunlun a landscape of life, resources and order. Luwu gives a visible body to the work of guarding and governing that immense imagined place.

**This platter first**

During the lunch rush, one raised paw establishes the serving order. Bifang resents interference. You perfect that piece, Luwu replies; I make sure the table eats together. Their argument generally ends with several hot plates leaving at once.

**Another view of a sacred mountain**

A traveller describes palaces on a holy mountain at home. Luwu asks who looks after them. Talk descends from cloud-dwelling gods to doors, steps and seasons. Kunlun gains the intimacy of a place where somebody works each day.

### 精卫 · Jīngwèi

精卫是白喙赤足的小鸟，也是小馆里不肯留下一张脏桌的服务员。它动作轻快，收拾完一处，又会回头检查最后一道水痕。

**海留下的名字**

《北山经》说炎帝之女女娃游东海而溺，化作精卫，常衔西山木石去填东海。白喙、赤足、花纹的头，把一场失落留成了可以看见的身影；海浪来回，鸟仍一次次折返。

**典籍里的身世**

精卫最初落在发鸠山上，山多柘木，鸟的形体像乌，头有花纹，白喙赤足，鸣声又像在自呼名字。她原是炎帝的少女女娃，游东海溺而不返，才化为衔石之鸟。原文把故乡、亲属、死亡与反复飞行连在一起，短短一段，既有形貌，也有完整的命运。山海里的许多兽以奇状留名，精卫却让读者同时记住一段伤痛，以及伤痛之后仍在继续的动作。

**还有最后一下**

它做事常说“就一下”，说完又多做一下。耳鼠替它把抹布藏起来，它便站在窗沿生气，直到一杯热水送到面前。喝水时精卫仍望着桌角，伙伴们只好先把那里擦净。

**木石的分量**

这则中国故事常被读成坚持，也容得下悲伤与不甘。远行的人在不同年纪听见它，会认出不同的海。小馆收工后的窗边留着一处空位，给终于肯停一停的那只鸟。

从发鸠山上的一只鸟，读到她曾经的名字与未完的远行。

> 又北二百里曰發鳩之山，其上多柘木。有鳥焉，其狀如烏，文首白喙赤足，名曰精衛，其鳴自詨。是炎帝之少女，名曰女娃。女娃遊于東海，溺而不返，故為精衛，常銜西山之木石，以堙于東海。
> ——《山海经·北山经》·发鸠之山
> Another two hundred li north is Mount Fajiu, where many zhe mulberries grow. There is a bird shaped like a crow, with a patterned head, white beak and red feet. It is called Jingwei, and its cry calls out its own name. It was the Flame Emperor's youngest daughter, named Nüwa. Nüwa went to the Eastern Sea, drowned and did not return; thus she became Jingwei. She is forever carrying wood and stones from the Western Mountains to fill the Eastern Sea.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Northern Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

发鸠山多柘树，有鸟像乌，头有花纹、白喙赤足，名为精卫，叫声像自己的名字。她原是炎帝的小女儿女娃，游东海溺亡，化鸟后常衔西山木石，要把东海填平。

**Jingwei, the Sea-Filling Bird**

Jingwei is a quick waiter with a white beak, red feet and a fierce objection to unfinished cleaning. Even an empty table receives one final inspection.

**The name the sea kept**

The Northern Mountains tells of Nüwa, daughter of the Flame Emperor, drowning in the Eastern Sea and becoming Jingwei. The bird carries wood and stone from the western mountains to fill the waters. Its repeated journeys give grief a visible shape.

**A life in the old books**

Jingwei first appears on Fajiu Mountain among mulberry trees: crow-like, patterned-headed, white-beaked and red-footed, calling its own name. The bird was Nüwa, the Flame Emperor's youngest daughter, who drowned in the Eastern Sea. Home, kinship, death and repeated flight occupy one compact narrative. Many creatures in the Classic are remembered through anatomy; Jingwei also leaves a complete fate, and an action that continues after loss. Reading the passage in full allows perseverance and grief to remain together.

**Just one more wipe**

Jingwei always promises one last task. Ershu hides the cloth; an indignant bird retreats to the window until somebody brings hot water. Its eyes remain on a damp corner, which the others quietly dry.

**The weight of a pebble**

Chinese readers have found perseverance in this tale, and sorrow too. At different ages, the same sea can mean something different. After closing, a place remains by the window for the bird that finally agrees to rest.

### 穷奇 · Qióngqí

穷奇掌麻辣锅，生着让人难忘的凶相，做菜也爱用重味。它下料果断，说话更直，客人若只夸“还行”，它能追问到门口。

**两条山路上的凶兽**

古书给穷奇留下不止一种模样：一处像牛而披刺毛，一处似虎而生翼；四凶的叙事又添上它助恶的名声。几条传说各有峻厉之处，合起来，才有这位令人不敢轻慢的山海来客。

**典籍里的身世**

穷奇的两副形貌，都能在《山海经》中找到位置：《西山经》邽山条说它似牛、披猬毛、声如嗥犬，《海内北经》又写它似虎有翼，威胁从地上伸向空中。《左传·文公十八年》则把穷奇列入四凶，以毁信废忠、亲近恶人为其恶名添重。外形在变，那份令人畏惧的性情一直鲜明。读这些不同讲法，如同绕着同一块嶙峋山石走一圈，每换一步，都又见出它一道锋利的边。

**这口锅有脾气**

它认定一锅好汤要让人立刻记住，花椒入油时眼神都亮起来。遇见吃不了辣的客人，它先皱眉，再认真另调一碗。端上去还要问：“这回呢？香总该闻得出来吧？”

**尝过再回答**

它听得懂许多种夸奖，却最服有来有回的意见：麻停在哪里，辣从哪一口开始，汤里还缺什么。客人比画着说，它便比画着尝。火气很大的厨师，竟能为一句具体的评语等得很久。

先看邽山那一位披着猬毛的穷奇。

> 又西二百六十里曰邽山，其上有獸焉，其狀如牛，蝟毛，名曰窮奇，音如獋狗，是食人。
> ——《山海经·西山经》·邽山
> Another two hundred and sixty li west is Mount Gui. An animal lives upon it, shaped like an ox, with a hedgehog's bristles. It is called Qiongqi, sounds like a howling dog, and eats people.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Western Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

邽山有兽像牛，披着猬刺般的毛，名叫穷奇，声音像嗥叫的犬，以人为食。

翻到另一篇，形貌又生出翅膀。

> 窮竒狀如虎，有翼，食人從首始。
> ——《山海经·海内北经》
> Qiongqi is shaped like a tiger and has wings. When it eats a person, it begins at the head.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Northern Regions Within the Seas

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

这一篇中的穷奇像虎，长着翅膀，食人时从头部开始。

**Qiongqi, the Untamed One**

Qiongqi runs the mala pot with a fearsome face and decisive seasoning. Its speech is equally direct. Call a dish merely fine and it may follow you to the door.

**Several fierce histories**

Old accounts describe an ox-like creature with stiff hair and a winged tiger-like form; the Four Perils tradition adds a troubling reputation. These different paths give Qiongqi a presence that demands attention before a single bowl is served.

**A life in the old books**

Two bodily forms already live within the Classic. On Gui Mountain in the Western Mountains, Qiongqi resembles an ox with hedgehog-like bristles and a hound's call. The Regions Within the Seas gives it a tiger's form and wings. The Zuo Tradition places the name among the Four Perils, associated with broken trust and affinity for wrongdoing. Each account adds an edge. Moving among them resembles walking around a jagged stone: another face of the same formidable name appears at each turn.

**A pot with a temper**

Peppercorns entering hot oil light up its eyes. A guest who cannot take chilli receives a frown, followed by a carefully adjusted bowl. Qiongqi still needs an answer: surely the fragrance comes through this time?

**Taste, then reply**

It respects precise criticism: where the tingling settles, when the heat begins, what the broth still lacks. Guest and cook describe sensations with words and gestures. For one useful observation, this impatient chef can wait surprisingly long.

### 梼杌 · Táowù

梼杌负责宣传，招牌上的一个字也能与人争半日。它认定的事很难松口，偏又记得每一位曾认真听它说完的人。

**名字里的棱角**

《左传》借梼杌写顽固难教的性情，后来的异兽叙事添上虎形、长尾等模样。那一身棱角仿佛从名字里长出来，远远站着，也像有一场尚未结束的争论跟在身后。

**典籍里的身世**

梼杌的名字先在人事里带着棱角。《左传》写颛顼氏的不才子，教导也不听，劝说也不明，天下人因而称他梼杌；《太平御览》所引《神异经》又把它画成西方荒野的巨兽，虎形人面、虎足猪牙，尾长一丈八尺。顽固的性情与凶猛的身体，在流传中逐渐贴合。一读它怎样得名，一看那条长尾拖过荒野，门前那个认准一句招牌便不肯改口的伙伴，也就更有了来处。

**招牌重写三回**

灌灌嫌字不响亮，讹兽嫌话不够巧，梼杌却坚持写“肉烤到透，饭盛得足”。争到最后，它索性搬凳子坐在门口，逐位问客人看懂了没有。那张招牌因此留下了许多次擦改的痕迹。

**把一句话说准**

遇见不同语言的客人，它会把原句写得更慢，等对方找来自己的说法。每改一处，都像拔下一根刺；可当两人终于指着同一盘菜点头，它又要郑重把那个新词抄进本子里。

梼杌先以难以教诲的性情得名。

> 顓頊氏有不才子，不可教訓，不知話言；告之則頑，舍之則嚚，傲很明德，以亂天常。天下之民謂之『檮杌』。
> ——《左传·文公十八年》
> The Zhuanxu clan had an unworthy son who could not be taught and would not understand counsel. When admonished he was obstinate; when left alone he was insolent. He defied those of shining virtue and disrupted heaven's order. The people of the world called him Taowu.

> — The Zuo Tradition · Duke Wen, Year 18

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

颛顼氏有不肖的后人，不能接受教诲与善言，顽固悖乱、傲慢凶狠，扰乱常道，天下人称他为梼杌。

性情之外，后来的类书还保存了这一副异兽形貌。

> 西方荒中有獸焉，狀如虎而身大，虎毛長尺，人面虎足，口有猪牙，尾長一丈八尺，名曰檮杌。
> ——《太平御览》卷九一三引《神异经》
> In the western wilderness there is an animal shaped like a tiger, but huge in body. Its tiger's fur is a chi long; it has a human face and tiger's feet, boar's tusks in its mouth, and a tail one zhang and eight chi long. Its name is Taowu.

> — Taiping Imperial Reader, 913 · quoting the Classic of Divine Marvels

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

西方荒野有兽，像虎而身躯巨大，虎毛很长，人面虎足，口有猪牙，尾巴长至一丈八尺，名为梼杌。

**Taowu, the Stubborn One**

Taowu handles publicity and can argue over a single signboard word all afternoon. It rarely yields, yet remembers everyone who has listened to its whole explanation.

**Edges in a name**

The Zuo Tradition associates Taowu with stubborn, unteachable character. Later creature tales add a tiger-like form and long tail. Its sharp outline seems to have grown from the name itself, with an unfinished argument following behind.

**A life in the old books**

Taowu's name first acquires sharpness in an account of human conduct. The Zuo Tradition describes Zhuanxu's unteachable descendant. The Taiping Imperial Reader, quoting the Classic of Divine Marvels, supplies a western wilderness beast: huge and tiger-like, human-faced, with tiger feet, boar tusks and an extraordinarily long tail. Character and anatomy gradually cling to the same name. Read the unyielding disposition, then imagine that tail crossing the waste, and the inn's stubborn sign-writer gains a much more substantial ancestry.

**The sign rewritten**

Guanguan wants louder words; Eshou wants cleverer ones. Taowu insists on meat cooked through and bowls filled properly. Eventually it sits outside and asks each guest what they understand. The board gathers a history of erasures.

**Getting a phrase right**

For a guest speaking another language, it writes slowly and waits for an answering phrase. Every alteration costs effort. When both finally point to the same dish, Taowu carefully copies the new word into its notebook.

### 混沌 · Hùndùn

混沌是爱试新味的厨师，案台常被它弄得一团热闹。它能对着一味陌生香料出神，也能把昨日失败的配方藏得谁都找不到。

**七日的寓言**

《庄子》写中央之帝浑沌待人甚厚，朋友想报答它，便替没有七窍的它每日凿一窍，七日后浑沌死了。寓言把一份热心放在令人心惊的结尾，让读者久久望着那个原本自在的生命。

**典籍里的身世**

《庄子·应帝王》让浑沌做中央之帝，南海的儵与北海的忽在它的地方相会，受了款待便想报德，七窍的悲剧由此生出。《太平御览》另引《神异经》，把同名异兽写在昆仑之西，犬形，有目不见、有耳不闻，常回转向天而笑。一位是寓言中的主人，一位在异兽叙述中徘徊。浑圆、未分与感官的缺落，让这个名字始终带着难以一眼看透的意味，也给试味的厨师留下许多想象。

**先留一处空**

试新菜时，它总空出一只碗，等最后想到的那点滋味。有时空碗等到打烊，谁问起，混沌便把头转向窗外；也有一夜忽然拍案，让全店都来尝一口尚未取名的汤。

**让陌生有自己的样子**

客人送来的香料，它先闻很久，再问原来怎样用。有人说留整粒，有人说先磨碎，案上便出现两只小碟。等香气各自升起，那份新奇也终于有了能被仔细聆听的声音。

把款待、报德与结局连起来，才看得见这则寓言的转折。

> 南海之帝為儵，北海之帝為忽，中央之帝為渾沌。儵與忽時相與遇於渾沌之地，渾沌待之甚善。
> ——《庄子·应帝王》
> The ruler of the southern sea was called Shu. The ruler of the northern sea was called Hu. The ruler of the central zone was called Hun Tun.

Shu and Hu often met on Hun Tun's territory, and being always well treated by him, determined to repay his kindness.

> — Zhuangzi · Responding to Emperors and Kings

> English: Herbert A. Giles · Chuang Tzu (1889)

南海之帝叫儵，北海之帝叫忽，中央之帝叫浑沌。儵与忽常在浑沌的地方相会，浑沌待他们很好。后文写二人为报答它，替它凿出七窍。

好意到了这里，忽然改变了故事的方向。

> 儵與忽謀報渾沌之德，曰：「人皆有七竅以視聽食息，此獨无有，嘗試鑿之。」日鑿一竅，七日而渾沌死。
> ——《庄子·应帝王》·寓言的后半
> They said, "All men have seven holes,--for seeing, hearing, eating, and breathing. Hun Tun alone has none. We will bore some for him."

So every day they bored one hole; but on the seventh day Hun Tun died.

> — Zhuangzi · Responding to Emperors and Kings

> English: Herbert A. Giles · Chuang Tzu (1889)

儵与忽想报答浑沌，说人都有七窍供视听饮食呼吸，唯独它没有，便试着替它凿开。每天一窍，七天后浑沌死去。

**Hundun, Before the World Took Shape**

Hundun experiments in a gloriously untidy kitchen. An unfamiliar spice can hold its attention for hours; yesterday's failed recipe vanishes just as mysteriously.

**Seven days**

Zhuangzi tells of Hundun, a generous host without the human body's seven openings. Grateful friends make one opening each day. On the seventh, Hundun dies. The abrupt ending leaves readers with a haunting image of a life once complete in its own form.

**A life in the old books**

In Zhuangzi's Responding to Emperors and Kings, Hundun rules the centre and welcomes Shu of the south and Hu of the north. Their wish to repay its kindness begins the tragedy of the seven openings. A different account preserved in the Taiping Imperial Reader describes a dog-like Hundun west of Kunlun, with unseeing eyes and unhearing ears, turning and laughing toward the sky. Host and strange beast inhabit separate narratives. Roundness, undivided being and unusual senses give the experimenting chef a name with several depths.

**An empty bowl**

Hundun always leaves one bowl empty for the flavour it has yet to discover. Sometimes it waits until closing. Sometimes a sudden shout summons everyone to taste a soup that still has no name.

**Listening to an ingredient**

A guest offers a spice. Hundun smells it, then asks how it is used at home. Whole, says one; ground, says another. Two dishes appear on the counter. Distinct fragrances rise, each given time to introduce itself.

### 獬豸 · Xièzhì

獬豸独角，善辨曲直，在小馆照看收银与客诉。它问话不急，等人把气发完，才把账单摊开：“从第一道菜说起。”

**把判断托给一只兽**

古人想象獬豸能辨争讼是非，独角抵向理曲的一方；獬豸冠也因此走入司法服饰。难以看见的公正，有了一种可以刻在冠上、立在门前的形貌，让人抬头便认得那份寄望。

**典籍里的身世**

獬豸的形貌在不同书里可近于牛，也可近于羊，共同的标记是独角。《太平御览》引《神异经》，说它见人争斗便抵触理曲者，听到议论便咬住不正者；又引《论衡》里皋陶使一角之羊辨罪的传说。《宋书·符瑞志》再把它与狱讼公平联系起来。兽角于是进入法与公正的象征世界。小馆里那句“从第一道菜说起”，背后也像站着许多代人盼望曲直得明的目光。

**茶凉之前**

客人与伙计各说一套，它便把点单、送菜、结账的经过逐项问清。讹兽想插一句漂亮话，常被那根角轻轻一挡。等桌上只剩事实，獬豸才把茶续热，让双方自己把话接下去。

**角与天平**

远客在纸上画了一架天平，獬豸在旁边添上独角。两种形象，一种衡量，一种辨曲，陪人追问同一件难事：如何让受委屈的话有处落下，让作出的判断有人愿意相信。

独角、形貌与辨曲直的动作，在这里有完整描写。

> 東北荒中有獸如牛，一角，毛青，四足似熊，忠直，見人鬪則觸不直，聞人論則咋不正，名曰獬豸，一名任法獸。
> ——《太平御览》卷八九〇引《神异经》
> In the northeastern wilderness there is an animal like an ox, with one horn, blue-green fur and four bear-like feet. Loyal and upright, it strikes the person in the wrong when it sees a fight, and bites the one whose argument is unjust when it hears a dispute. Its name is Xiezhi; it is also called the beast that upholds the law.

> — Taiping Imperial Reader, 890

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

东北荒野有兽像牛，独角青毛，四足似熊，性情忠直；见人争斗便抵触理曲者，听人议论便咬住不正者，名獬豸，又叫任法兽。

另一种形貌把独角之羊带到皋陶的审判之前。

> 獬豸者一角之羊，性識有罪。臯繇治獄，有罪者令羊觸之。
> ——《太平御览》卷八九〇引《论衡》
> Xiezhi is a sheep with one horn. By nature it recognises the guilty. When Gaoyao judged a case, he had the sheep strike the guilty person.

> — Taiping Imperial Reader, 890

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

獬豸在这条传说里是一角之羊，能识有罪。皋陶治狱时，使羊以角抵触有罪者。

**Xiezhi, the One-Horned Judge**

Xiezhi is a one-horned judge of disputes and the inn's patient keeper of complaints. Once tempers settle, it unfolds the bill: begin with the first dish.

**A visible hope of justice**

Old accounts give Xiezhi the power to identify the party in the wrong. Its image entered judicial headwear. An elusive hope, fair judgment, acquired a horn and a recognisable shape that could stand above an official's brow.

**A life in the old books**

Xiezhi appears as ox-like in some accounts and sheep-like in others; the single horn remains its distinguishing sign. The Taiping Imperial Reader quotes a beast that strikes the party in the wrong and recalls the story of Gaoyao asking a one-horned sheep to distinguish guilt. The Book of Song associates its arrival with fairly resolved lawsuits. The horn therefore enters the imagery of law and judgment. Behind the inn's patient questions stands a long history of hoping that right and wrong might become discernible.

**Before the tea cools**

Order, delivery, payment: Xiezhi asks about each in turn. An elegant interruption from Eshou meets a gently lowered horn. When the sequence is clear, fresh tea arrives and both sides can resume speaking.

**A horn beside a balance**

A visitor draws scales; Xiezhi adds its horn. Weighing and discerning take different visible forms. Together they open an old question across languages: where can a grievance be heard, and how does a judgment earn trust?

### 青耕 · Qīng Gēng

青耕青身白尾，像一只清爽的鹊，做服务时总把桌边的小事看在眼里。它嗓音轻，却敢在满堂喧闹里替久候的客人问一句。

**把平安唤出来**

《中山经》说堇理山有青耕，白喙、白目、白尾，鸣声如自呼其名，又能御疫。小小山鸟托住了很大的盼望；青白相间的羽色，也像山中忽然吹来一阵清风。

**典籍里的身世**

青耕的堇理山多松柏、美梓，山阴藏丹雘与金，豹虎出没其间。这只青白相间、形似鹊的鸟，便从这样的山林中鸣叫出场。原文把青身、白喙、白目、白尾一一列出，再写“可以御疫”“其鸣自叫”，使形貌、声音与平安的愿望相连。中国古人常借异鸟记下对灾疾的忧虑与盼望，青耕把这份心思收得轻巧，一声名字，像给沉重山林递来一句清亮的问候。

**还差几口锅**

它最不爱听人敷衍地说“马上就好”，一定要到后厨问清楚。若厨师不耐烦，青耕就站在传菜口等，白尾一动不动。等到准信，才回来给客人续茶，把话说得简短而确切。

**一声名字，一声问候**

它教远客念Qīnggēng时，先指青色的羽，再轻轻唱出鸣声。有人跟着唱歪了调，青耕便重新唱一回。平安的古老愿望，在这两声不太齐的问候里变得轻巧可闻。

青白配色、自呼其名与御疫之愿，在原文里彼此相连。

> 有鳥焉，其狀如鵲，青身白喙白目白尾，名曰青耕，可以禦疫，其鳴自叫。
> ——《山海经·中山经》·堇理之山
> There is a bird shaped like a magpie, with a blue-green body and white beak, white eyes and a white tail. It is called Qinggeng, and it can ward off pestilence. Its cry calls out its own name.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Central Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

有鸟形似鹊，青身、白喙、白目、白尾，名叫青耕。古人赋予它御疫的愿望，其鸣声又像在呼唤自己的名字。

**Qinggeng, the Bird of Reassurance**

Qinggeng is blue-green with white markings, a magpie-like waiter attentive to overlooked tables. Its soft voice can still ask a firm question for somebody waiting.

**Calling for well-being**

The Central Mountains describes a bird with white beak, eyes and tail, calling its own name and warding off pestilence. A small mountain creature carries a large hope. Its clear colours resemble a breeze crossing the trees.

**A life in the old books**

Qinggeng's Jinli Mountain has pines, cypresses, fine trees, pigments and metals, with leopards and tigers moving through it. A magpie-like bird enters this landscape in blue-green and white. Beak, eyes and tail are carefully distinguished before the text connects its self-naming call with protection from pestilence. Appearance and sound carry an old hope of well-being. Among the Classic's heavier anxieties about disaster and illness, Qinggeng leaves a small, clear greeting that can still be heard beside the inn's tables.

**How many pots ahead?**

Qinggeng dislikes a vague almost ready and goes to the kitchen for an answer. Impatient chefs find a motionless white tail at the hatch until they explain. The waiter returns with tea and a short, accurate reply.

**A name as a greeting**

Teaching Qīnggēng begins with a green feather and a sung call. A visitor follows in an uncertain tune; Qinggeng sings again. The old wish for well-being becomes audible in two imperfectly matching greetings.

### 天禄 · Tiān Lù

天禄是招徕与收银的伙伴，瑞兽般的身影总爱站在门前亮处。它喜欢好兆头，遇上第一位客人，更要把招牌擦得格外干净。

**珠玉间的一只兽**

天禄作为瑞兽走进古人的装饰，《后汉书》所记步摇六兽里，也有写作“天鹿”的名字。兽形与珠玉一起随步轻动，把福禄之愿佩在身上；一段古老祝福，也便有了可以看见的轮廓。

**典籍里的身世**

《后汉书·舆服志》把“天鹿”列在步摇六兽之中：黄金作山形，白珠如桂枝，兽形又以翡翠装点，随人行步轻摇。另一处宫廷记载还说铸作天禄，把瑞兽的名字留进铜器与殿宇之间。珠玉、服饰与宫阙为它添上可见的华彩，也使福禄之愿有了随身和立于门前的形貌。小馆门口那一声迎客，身后仿佛还轻轻响着古人头饰上相碰的白珠。

**今日会有好消息**

它把第一声夸奖转告厨房，顺手把歪掉的菜单摆正。可若下雨误了生意，天禄也会闷闷不乐。貔貅把账算完递给它，它才发现：原来今日照样有几件值得记住的好事。

**带在身边的愿望**

有人把门上的纹样画给它看，有人摘下腕间旧挂饰。天禄逐件欣赏，见到兽形更要细问名字。各地的祝福在灯下轻轻碰响，像步摇上那几枚随着人一起走远的珠玉。

兽名在珠玉与金饰之间，成为可以佩戴的形象。

> 步摇以黄金为山题，贯白珠为桂枝相缪，一爵九华，熊、虎、赤罴、天鹿、辟邪、南山丰大特六兽。
> ——《后汉书·舆服志》·步摇六兽
> The buyao headdress uses gold to form a mountain crest and strings white pearls into intertwining cassia branches: one bird with nine blossoms, and six beasts—a bear, tiger, red bear, Tianlu, Bixie, and the great bull of the Southern Mountain.

> — The Book of the Later Han · Dress and Carriages

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

步摇以黄金制成山形，以白珠串成相缠的桂枝，其间装饰鸟与六兽，天鹿便列在这些兽形之中。

名字又被铸进宫廷的铜器世界。

> 复修玉堂殿，铸铜人四，黄钟四，及天禄、虾蟆，又铸四出文钱。
> ——《后汉书·孝灵帝纪》·中平三年
> Yutang Hall was repaired again. Four bronze human figures and four great bells were cast, as well as Tianlu and toads; coins bearing four radiating strokes were also cast.

> — The Book of the Later Han · Emperor Ling

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

这段宫廷记事说，修复玉堂殿，又铸铜人、黄钟、天禄与虾蟆等物，并铸钱币。

**Tianlu, the Auspicious Companion**

Tianlu welcomes guests and works the till, an auspicious creature happiest in the bright doorway. For the first arrival, it gives the sign an especially careful polish.

**A creature among jewels**

The Book of the Later Han records a creature written Tianlu among figures on a moving headdress. Auspicious animal forms travelled through ornament, carrying wishes for fortune with every step. An old blessing acquired a shape that could be worn.

**A life in the old books**

The Book of the Later Han places a creature written Tianlu among six animals on a moving headdress. Gold forms a mountain, pearls become interlacing branches and jade-like feathers adorn the figures. Another palace account records Tianlu among cast objects. Ornament, bronze and architecture give the auspicious name material lives. A wish for fortune can be worn or placed beside a building. Behind the inn's greeting, one can almost hear the small movement of pearls on an ancient headdress.

**Good news today**

Tianlu carries the first compliment to the kitchen and straightens the menu. Rain-soaked, quiet days dampen its spirits. Pixiu's finished accounts help: several small achievements have survived the weather and deserve remembering.

**Wishes worn abroad**

A guest sketches a pattern from a doorway; another removes an old pendant. Tianlu admires each and asks the creatures' names. Different blessings softly meet under the lamp, like jewels travelling with their wearer.

### 凤凰 · Fènghuáng

凤凰五彩而善歌舞，来到小馆后，灶边、桌旁都能见到它。它爱惜羽毛，也爱惜体面，最忙时仍要把衣襟上一点汤渍擦净。

**丹穴山的纹章**

《南山经》写丹穴山的凤凰，羽纹与德、义、礼、仁、信相联，自歌自舞，见则天下安宁。五彩在古人的笔下有了品格，百鸟之美也系着一份世间太平的愿望。

**典籍里的身世**

丹穴山条把凤凰的羽纹逐处展开：首为德、翼为义、背为礼、胸为仁、腹为信，身体像一卷可以读的纹章。《宋书·符瑞志》又称它仁鸟，把它的到来与珍惜生命相连，并细写鸣声、行止和群鸟相从的景象。凤凰的华美因而一直牵着秩序、品格与太平的愿望。看它在灯下理顺羽翼，便仿佛那些古老的字也随着五彩，慢慢回到人间。

**把灯一盏盏点好**

它嫌伙伴们把欢迎词念得敷衍，常拉住灌灌重教一回。可午市忙乱起来，凤凰也会卷起衣袖替人收盘。末班收工，它把最后一盏灯拨正，才肯坐下理顺折起的羽翼。

**两种鸟，两个名字**

远客说起phoenix，带来灰烬里再生的火光；Fenghuang的歌里，则回荡着中国古老的安宁之愿。两种名字写在同一页，火与五彩相照，为一碗热羹添上各自不同的余韵。

把五彩的纹理读完，才看见凤凰身上那些有名字的品格。

> 有鳥焉，其狀如雞，五采而文，名曰鳳凰。首文曰徳，翼文曰義，背文曰禮，膺文曰仁，腹文曰信。是鳥也飲食自然，自歌自舞，見則天下安寧。
> ——《山海经·南山经》·丹穴之山
> There is a bird shaped like a chicken, patterned in five colours, called Fenghuang. The marking on its head reads Virtue, on its wings Righteousness, on its back Ritual, on its breast Benevolence, and on its belly Trust. This bird eats and drinks naturally, singing and dancing of its own accord. When it appears, peace reigns throughout the world.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Southern Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

有鸟像鸡而五彩有纹，名凤凰。头、翼、背、胸、腹上的纹分别联系德、义、礼、仁、信；它自然饮食，自歌自舞，出现时天下安宁。

祥瑞的意义又与善待生命相接。

> 鳳凰者，仁鳥也，不刳胎剖卵則至。
> ——《宋书·符瑞志》·凤凰
> Fenghuang is a bird of benevolence. It comes when unborn creatures are not cut out and eggs are not broken open.

> — The Book of Song · Auspicious Signs

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

凤凰被称为仁鸟；这条记载把它的到来，与不伤害胎生、卵生的幼小生命联系起来。

**Fenghuang, the Auspicious Bird**

Fenghuang brings five-coloured plumage and a fondness for ceremony to every job in the inn. Even during a rush, it notices a splash on its collar.

**Markings on Danxue Mountain**

The Southern Mountains connects Fenghuang's patterned body with virtues and its song and dance with peace. Beauty acquires a moral and social dimension: the magnificent bird carries a wish for the world to flourish in harmony.

**A life in the old books**

The Danxue passage gives Fenghuang much more than an auspicious arrival. Head, wings, back, breast and belly bear markings named for virtue, righteousness, ritual, benevolence and trust. Its body becomes a readable emblem. The Book of Song calls it a benevolent bird and connects its appearance with care for life, alongside elaborate accounts of calls and movements. Beauty remains attached to qualities and peaceful order. As the inn's bird arranges its feathers, those old words seem to settle again among their colours.

**Straighten the final lamp**

Fenghuang makes Guanguan rehearse a careless welcome, then rolls up its own sleeves to clear the lunch dishes. At closing, it straightens the last lamp before sitting down to arrange its folded wings.

**Two names on a page**

A traveller's phoenix brings the fire of rebirth from ashes. Fenghuang's song carries an old Chinese promise of peace. Written together, the two names let flame and coloured feathers illuminate distinct hopes beside the same warm bowl.

### 白泽 · Báizé

白泽是小馆的导师，能说万物旧事，也肯陪新伙计重做一盘炒饭。它随身的本子页角卷起，空白处常留着后来补上的问题。

**给未知一个名字**

黄帝遇见能言白泽、向它问知万物的故事，使这位神兽与博识相连；《白泽图》的线索又把知识留在图像与名字之间。那些曾令旅人不安的异影，一旦可以辨认，也就有了相处的开端。

**典籍里的身世**

《抱朴子·登涉》提到《白泽图》，把辨识鬼神的名字、形貌视为远行入山的知识；《云笈七签·轩辕本纪》则写黄帝东巡海滨遇白泽，神兽能言，讲出万物之情与一万一千五百二十种鬼神变化，黄帝命人绘图传示。一个是图册的线索，一个是问知成书的故事，合在一起，便看出白泽为何成为博识的象征。它的力量在于使无名的异影有名可认，让走向陌生山川的人，能够多带上一页知识。

**把锅铲交回去**

新伙计炒坏了饭，白泽先让它尝，再问是米太湿还是火太急。讲完以后，锅铲仍交回对方手里。它记得每个伙伴学会第一道菜的样子，偏不肯在自己的本子上写“教完了”。

**还有一个问题**

来客带来异乡的动植物名字，白泽也会停笔请教。有人读得快，它便重问一遍，把音节记在页边。等客人告辞，那本看似什么都知道的书，又悄悄厚了一点。

海滨相遇以后，白泽的讲述被画成可供世人辨识的图。

> 帝巡狩東至海，登桓山，於海濱得白澤神獸，能言，達於萬物之情。因問天下鬼神之事，自古精氣爲物、逰魂爲變者，凡萬一千五百二十種。白澤言之，帝令以圖寫之，以示天下。
> ——《云笈七签·轩辕本纪》
> The emperor travelled east on inspection to the sea and climbed Mount Huan. Beside the sea he encountered the divine beast Baize, which could speak and understood the nature of all things. He asked it about the spirits of the world: beings formed from vital essences and transformations of wandering souls since ancient times, eleven thousand five hundred and twenty kinds in all. Baize described them, and the emperor ordered pictures made to show the world.

> — Yunji Qiqian · Xuanyuan Benji

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

黄帝东巡至海，在海滨遇见能言、通晓万物的白泽，问它鬼神变化之事。白泽讲出一万一千五百二十种，黄帝便命人绘图，传示天下。

另一部书把《白泽图》放在入山识异的知识之中。

> 其次則論百鬼録，知天下鬼之名字，及白澤圖、九鼎記，則衆鬼自却。
> ——《抱朴子·内篇·登涉》
> Next, by studying the Records of the Hundred Spirits, knowing the names of the world's spirits, and consulting the Baize Illustrations and the Record of the Nine Tripods, one can cause the many spirits to retreat of their own accord.

> — Baopuzi · Entering Mountains and Crossing Waters

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

书中把研读鬼神名录、《白泽图》和《九鼎记》，写作识别鬼神、使其退避的办法。名字与图像，都是远行者理解山中异影的凭借。

**Baize, Keeper of Many Stories**

Baize teaches the inn's staff and tells the histories of countless beings. Its notebook has curled corners and questions squeezed into the remaining margins.

**A name for the unknown**

Stories of the Yellow Emperor questioning a speaking Baize connect the creature with knowledge of the world. Traces of Baize illustrations join pictures to names. Something once frightening on a traveller's path becomes a being that can be recognised.

**A life in the old books**

The Baopuzi mentions the Baize illustrations among knowledge for entering mountains and recognising spirits. The Yunji Qiqian tells of the Yellow Emperor meeting a speaking Baize by the sea, questioning it about eleven thousand five hundred and twenty kinds of spirits and transformations, then ordering illustrations to be made. One text leaves traces of a diagrammatic tradition; another supplies a story of inquiry and recorded knowledge. Together they explain the learned creature's enduring appeal: unknown shapes acquire names, and a traveller can carry another page of understanding into unfamiliar country.

**Return the spatula**

A failed plate of rice begins a lesson in tasting: wet grains or hurried heat? After the explanation, Baize hands the spatula back. It remembers each learner's first successful dish, yet never marks the teaching finished.

**One more question**

Visitors bring unfamiliar plant and animal names. Baize asks them to repeat the sounds and writes them carefully. By departure, the supposedly all-knowing book has become a little thicker.

### 应龙 · Yìnglóng

应龙有翼，掌水与雨，如今在小馆烹调水产。它做菜惯于先听锅声，窗外若响起第一滴雨，翅膀便会不自觉地舒展开来。

**雨落以前**

《山海经》里的应龙参与黄帝与蚩尤的战争，也与蓄水、旱雨相连。久旱时，人们借应龙的形象盼雨。那双翅膀越过战场，也掠过田垄，最终停在人们仰望云层的目光里。

**典籍里的身世**

应龙在《大荒北经》走入黄帝与蚩尤的战争，先蓄水，又与风伯、雨师及女魃的旱雨相争连在一起。《大荒东经》再写它杀蚩尤与夸父后不能返天，地上因而屡旱，人们仿其形便得大雨；另一篇又说它居南方，使南方多雨。同一位龙神的身影，随着几段叙述在战场、天界与南方水土间转移。雨从何来、旱为何起，被古人写成一场有力量也有遗憾的远行。

**锅里也有江河**

它下水产时手很轻，最怕别人把清鲜煮成一锅急躁。有人问雨何时停，应龙往窗外看一眼，只回答海鲜该何时起锅。天上的事太远，碗里的这一口，它倒愿意管到底。

**龙的另一条水路**

远客说家乡故事里的龙守着洞窟和宝藏，应龙便讲起求雨时的田野。话题顺着不同的传说走，落到同样干裂的泥土。原来一场及时雨，也足够让龙的故事在人心里长久盘旋。

离开天界的经历，与人间的求雨习俗连在一起。

> 應龍處南極，殺蚩尤與夸父，不得復上，故下數旱，旱而為應龍之狀，乃得大雨。
> ——《山海经·大荒东经》
> Yinglong dwells in the southern extremity. It killed Chiyou and Kuafu and could not return above; therefore drought often occurs below. In drought, people make the likeness of Yinglong, and then abundant rain falls.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Eastern Wilderness

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

应龙居于南方尽处，杀蚩尤与夸父以后不能再上天，地上于是常旱；天旱时，人们做出应龙的形象，才得到大雨。

另一段旧事把它留在多雨的南方。

> 應龍已殺蚩尤，又殺夸父，乃去南方處之，故南方多雨。
> ——《山海经·大荒北经》
> After Yinglong killed Chiyou and then Kuafu, it went to dwell in the south. This is why the south has much rain.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Northern Wilderness

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

应龙杀蚩尤与夸父后，离去而居南方，因此南方多雨。

**Yinglong, the Rain-Bearing Dragon**

Yinglong has wings and a history of water and rain. At the inn, this seafood chef listens to the pot and stretches unconsciously at the first drops outside.

**Before the rain**

In the Classic of Mountains and Seas, Yinglong joins the Yellow Emperor's battles and becomes associated with stored water, drought and rainmaking. Its wings pass over conflict and fields alike, arriving in the upward gaze of people waiting for clouds.

**A life in the old books**

Yinglong enters the Yellow Emperor's battle with Chiyou in the Northern Wilderness, storing water amid a conflict involving wind, rain and drought. The Eastern Wilderness tells of its inability to return to heaven and of people seeking rain through its likeness. Another passage places it in the rainy south. Across these accounts, the dragon moves between battlefield, sky and regional landscape. Questions about rain and drought acquire the shape of a powerful journey with loss as well as victory.

**Rivers in the pot**

Yinglong handles seafood gently and dislikes a delicate broth boiled into haste. Asked when the rain will stop, it answers when supper should leave the heat. The sky is vast; this particular bowl is its responsibility.

**Another dragon's waterway**

A visitor remembers treasure-guarding dragons. Yinglong describes rain awaited over farmland. Different tales lead their conversation toward the same cracked earth. Timely rain supplies another enduring reason to imagine a dragon.

### 烛龙 · Zhúlóng

烛龙人面蛇身，通体赤色，传说睁眼为昼、闭眼为夜。它在食肆掌勺，最爱夜市开灯时刻，却总嫌客人把面催得太急。

**钟山的一次呼吸**

《海外北经》写钟山之神烛阴，视为昼，瞑为夜；《大荒北经》又有烛龙的赤色长身。昼夜与季候借着一双眼、一息风变得可以看见，天地的运转也有了一位缓缓醒来的主人。

**典籍里的身世**

烛龙的两段原文各有壮阔。《海外北经》称钟山之神烛阴，睁眼为昼、闭眼为夜，吹气为冬、呼气为夏，身长千里，人面蛇身而赤；《大荒北经》又把烛龙置于赤水以北的章尾山，以目光明暗写天地。眼睛、呼吸和躯体都成了宇宙的尺度。夜市里那双凝望汤锅的眼，背后曾开合过整片天空；一只碗里的热气，也便与古人想象季候的那一息风遥遥相接。

**面还没有熟**

它记时很准，收火却从不迁就催促。客人笑问能否眨眨眼把等待变短，烛龙便把锅盖揭给人看：“你看，它也得自己醒。”说完慢慢合盖，留一缕白汽向夜色里升去。

**把天空讲近些**

人们曾借太阳、车驾、神鸟或巨蛇讲述天光来去。烛龙的故事把日夜系在呼吸与睁眼之间。夜食的人听着听着，也会留意自己掌心的热碗，和窗外正在一点点变深的蓝。

昼夜之外，呼吸还连着冬夏与风。

> 鍾山之神，名曰燭隂，視為晝，暝為夜，吹為冬，呼為夏，不飲不食不息，息為風，身長千里。
> ——《山海经·海外北经》·钟山之神
> The god of Mount Zhong is named Zhuyin. When it looks, there is day; when it closes its eyes, there is night. Its blowing makes winter, its exhaling summer. It neither drinks nor eats nor breathes; when it does breathe, there is wind. Its body is a thousand li long.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Beyond the Northern Seas

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

钟山之神名叫烛阴，睁眼为昼、闭眼为夜，吹气为冬、呼气为夏，不饮不食，气息又成风，身躯长达千里。

赤色长身与明暗的神力，在另一篇中再度显现。

> 有神，人面蛇身而赤，直目正乘，其暝乃晦，其視乃明，不食不寢不息，風雨是謁，是燭九隂，是謂燭龍。
> ——《山海经·大荒北经》·章尾山
> There is a god with a human face and a red serpent's body, its eyes set in a straight gaze. When its eyes close, all is dark; when it looks, all is bright. It neither eats nor sleeps nor breathes; wind and rain attend it. It illuminates the Nine Darknesses, and is called Zhulong.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Northern Wilderness

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

章尾山有神，人面、赤色蛇身，目光直视；闭目则昏暗，睁眼则光明，不食不眠，以风雨为其神异之一，照见九阴，名为烛龙。

**Zhulong, Keeper of Day and Night**

Zhulong is red-bodied and serpent-shaped, with a human face. Its eyes govern day and night in old tales; its present concern is a noodle pot that guests keep rushing.

**A breath over Zhong Mountain**

The Classic describes Zhuyin making day by looking and night by closing its eyes, and elsewhere a red Zhulong. Changing light and seasons acquire a body, a gaze and a breath: the sky becomes something a listener can picture.

**A life in the old books**

Two passages give Zhulong a cosmic scale. Beyond the Northern Seas, the god Zhuyin makes day and night by opening and closing its eyes, winter and summer through its breath. Its red, human-faced serpent body stretches a thousand li. The Northern Wilderness places Zhulong on Zhangwei Mountain and again ties light to its gaze. Eyes, breath and body become measures of the world. Behind the chef watching a noodle pot lies a presence whose movements once opened and closed an entire sky.

**The noodles are still asleep**

A guest asks whether one blink could shorten the wait. Zhulong lifts the lid: these must wake by themselves. Steam rises slowly into the evening as it closes the pot again.

**Bringing the sky closer**

Sun chariots, birds and great serpents carry daylight through different traditions. Zhulong ties it to breathing and sight. Listening over supper, a guest notices the warm bowl in their hands and the blue deepening outside.

### 帝江 · Dìjiāng

帝江形似赤色黄囊，六足四翼，浑敦无面目，却识得歌舞。它负责传菜，轻轻一转身，托盘就像踩上了旁人听不见的拍子。

**天山自有乐声**

《西山经》把帝江安放在天山，那里有金玉，也有向西南流去的水。六足、四翼与无面目的形体被写在一处，最后忽然落下“识歌舞”三字，仿佛古书自己也听见了节拍。

**典籍里的身世**

帝江住在金玉、青雄黄与英水相伴的天山。原文先说它像黄囊，又说赤如丹火，六足四翼，浑敦而无面目，最后以“识歌舞”收束。一连串关于身体的描写，竟由音乐把它们连在了一起。它也因此常被读者联想到浑圆未分的“混沌”意象：一具没有眉眼的形体，仍能感知节奏、参与欢舞。小馆那一下等客人接稳盘子的停顿，便像把古书里未曾写出声音的一拍，轻轻续了下去。

**这一拍请接稳**

它端菜时总等客人双手接好，才转入下一步。灌灌想替它配一声响亮的报菜，帝江却用六只脚敲出更细的节奏。连最心急的一桌，也会跟着那一下停顿，把碗稳稳放好。

**听见一个动作**

陌生语言尚未来得及译完，鞠躬、让路与递盘已经完成。帝江的欢迎没有眉眼，却有起落与回应。远客学着它的步子离开，门口响起一串略显笨拙、又十分认真的足音。

从天山水土，到无面目的身体，再到歌舞，帝江的出场自有节奏。

> 有神焉，其狀如黄囊，赤如丹火，六足四翼，渾敦無面目，是識歌舞，實惟帝江也。
> ——《山海经·西山经》·天山
> There is a god shaped like a yellow pouch, red as cinnabar fire, with six feet and four wings. Round and without a face or eyes, it understands song and dance. This is indeed Dijiang.

> — The Classic of Mountains and Seas · Western Mountains

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

天山有神，形似黄囊，颜色像丹火般赤红，六足四翼，浑敦无面目，却识得歌舞，这就是帝江。

**Dijiang, the Faceless Dancer**

Dijiang is red as flame, with six feet, four wings and no distinct face. It knows song and dance, and carries dishes to a rhythm only it seems to hear.

**Music on Tianshan**

The Western Mountains places Dijiang among Tianshan's gold, jade and flowing waters. Its startling anatomy concludes with an unexpected accomplishment: it understands music and dance. The old description seems to discover a beat of its own.

**A life in the old books**

Dijiang belongs to Tianshan, among gold, jade, orpiment and the Yingshui. Its body resembles a yellow pouch yet glows red as cinnabar fire, with six feet, four wings and no distinct face. The description then discovers music and dance. Sound and movement gather its extraordinary anatomy into a living presence. The rounded, undivided form has invited comparisons with Hundun imagery. In the inn, a pause while the guest receives a plate can seem to continue the unwritten beat behind that ancient description.

**Catch this beat**

Dijiang waits for a guest to receive the plate before turning. Guanguan offers a loud announcement; six feet answer with a finer rhythm. Even an impatient table pauses at the right moment and sets the bowl safely down.

**An action understood**

Before a translation is finished, the bow, the offered space and the dish have arrived. This welcome has timing instead of facial expression. Guests leave trying its steps, with earnest, slightly clumsy footfalls at the door.

### 九婴 · Jiǔyīng

九婴是多首的厨师，能同时照看几处灶火。忙起来每个脑袋都觉得自己最清醒，厨房里最热闹的争论，有时只来自它一个。

**凶水上的旧名**

《淮南子》记羿在凶水之上杀九婴，古注称它为水火之怪。后来流传的多首形象，把相反的水与火聚在同一个名字里。那些猛烈的力量，在旧故事里曾让人畏惧。

**典籍里的身世**

《淮南子·本经训》把九婴放在十日并出、禾稼焦枯的灾世，猰貐、大风、封豨与修蛇也都为民害。尧使羿除害，九婴便在凶水之上被杀。高诱注用“水火之怪”点出它的力量，简短的正文与注语，留下了足够猛烈的轮廓。后来常见的多首形象，把这种难以兼顾的力量画得更加热闹。灶边几口锅同时醒来的时候，仿佛仍能看见水与火挤在一个名字里。

**几口锅，几种意见**

这一锅该添汤，那一锅要收汁，九婴能同时看顾，也能同时嫌别人手慢。等几只锅一齐沸起，它又得请伙伴接盘。最爱逞强的那颗头，这时候偏偏把声音放得最轻。

**多首的各自旧事**

别处也有多首怪物，有的守门，有的盘踞水泽。九婴的名字却沿着羿与凶水走来。初见相似的轮廓，旅人便问起它的故事；听到末尾，灶上又多了一口正等出菜的热锅。

九婴的名字，要放回十日并出与羿除害的故事里读。

> 逮至堯之時，十日並出，焦禾稼，殺草木，而民無所食。猰貐、鑿齒、九嬰、大風、封豨、脩蛇，皆為民害。堯乃使羿誅鑿齒於疇華之野，殺九嬰於㐫水之上。
> ——《淮南子·本经训》·羿除害
> In Yao's time, ten suns appeared together. They scorched the grain, killed the plants and trees, and left the people nothing to eat. Yayu, Zaochi, Jiuying, Great Wind, Fengxi and the Long Serpent all brought harm to the people. Yao therefore sent Yi to slay Zaochi on the plain of Chouhua, and to kill Jiuying beside Fierce Water.

> — Huainanzi · Benjing

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

尧时十日并出，禾稼草木受害，百姓无食；九婴与其他凶物又一同为害。尧命羿除害，羿在畴华之野诛凿齿，在凶水之上杀九婴。

古注以水与火，点明这个名字的凶烈。

> 九嬰水火之怪為人害。
> ——《淮南子·本经训》·高诱注
> Jiuying is a monster of water and fire that brings harm to people.

> — Huainanzi · Benjing · Gao You commentary

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

九婴是与水火相连、危害人的异怪。

**Jiuying, Many Heads at the Stove**

Jiuying watches several cooking stations at once. Each head considers itself the most alert; some of the kitchen's liveliest arguments involve only this one chef.

**A name at Fierce Water**

The Huainanzi records Yi slaying Jiuying, with old commentary calling it a creature of water and fire. Later many-headed imagery gathers opposing forces into one formidable being. Its name still carries the turbulence of that story.

**A life in the old books**

The Huainanzi places Jiuying in an age when ten suns scorch crops and several monsters afflict the people. Yao sends Yi to remove these dangers; Jiuying falls at Fierce Water. Gao You's commentary identifies it as a creature of water and fire. Brief text and annotation leave a forceful outline, later enriched by familiar many-headed imagery. Several simultaneously boiling pots make those competing elements easy to picture, gathered into one crowded name beside the kitchen's fires.

**Several pots, several opinions**

One pot needs broth, another reduction. Jiuying handles both and complains about slow hands until everything boils at once. Then it needs help with the plates. The proudest head makes the quietest request.

**Each many-headed history**

Other traditions have many-headed creatures at gates and in waters. Jiuying's story follows Yi and Fierce Water. A traveller begins with a familiar silhouette and discovers a different tale while another pot reaches the serving point.

## 一桌山海

### 阳春面 · Yáng chūn miàn

清汤托着细面，葱香先于筷子升起。阳春面是有间小馆开门便有的一碗，几样寻常滋味，接住一天里最早的饥饿。

**清鲜的底子**

江南常见的阳春面，也叫光面，热汤、面条与调味便能成一餐。汤要清，滋味却要站得住；面要顺，入口仍留一点弹性。《随园食单》所说“清鲜”二字，恰好让这份简净有了分量。

**葱花浮起来**

羬羊尝过汤才肯落面，鸮端起碗便不再停步。葱花从碗边散开，第一筷面挑起来时，汤面还留着一圈轻轻晃动的油光。早到的客人常先不说话，只把热气慢慢吸进鼻尖。

**鲜字的第一课**

初尝中国汤面的旅人，往往先问配料在哪里。喝到碗底，才发现清汤也能留下长长的余味。Xian这个陌生音节，从一口鲜香开始，渐渐有了可以带回家讲给人听的形状。

> 味要清鲜，不可淡薄。
> ——《随园食单》
> Flavour should be clear and fresh, never thin or insipid.

> English: English rendering · Shanhai Inn

味道要清鲜，仍须留得住滋味。

**Yangchun Noodles**

Clear broth, fine noodles and spring onion introduce the day at Youjian. Yangchun noodles are available from opening, a familiar warmth for the first hungry guest.

**The weight of simplicity**

Often called plain noodles, this everyday Jiangnan bowl relies on broth, seasoning and texture. Clear soup needs depth; smooth strands keep a little spring. The Suiyuan Shidan's ideal of clear, fresh flavour gives this modest meal a demanding standard.

**When the garnish opens**

Qianyang tastes the broth before the noodles enter. Xiao carries the bowl directly to the table. Spring onion spreads across a faint sheen, and the first lifted strands release a breath of fragrant steam.

**Learning xian**

A traveller first looks for elaborate toppings. By the final spoonful, the broth has supplied its own lingering answer. Xian, savoury freshness, becomes a Chinese word with a flavour to take home.

### 蛋炒饭 · Dàn chǎo fàn

米粒在热锅里散开，鸡蛋裹上金黄，葱香随锅铲一翻而起。蛋炒饭是小馆最家常的主食，也最容易叫人认真谈起自己的家。

**粒粒之间**

熟米饭下锅，要松散，又要留着米香；鸡蛋与饭粒相遇，有人爱裹得均匀，有人偏爱见得着的嫩块。各家的手势不同，一盘炒饭便在火候、调味与习惯之间，生出许多亲近的版本。

**锅铲响三声**

忙时它出得利落，盛到盘里仍有热气钻出。伙计端饭经过门口，香气常能让正在看招牌的人转过头。赶路的客人坐下吃几口，肩上的行囊也跟着慢慢放低。

**你家的那一盘**

有人想起加豌豆的饭，有人想起昨日余下的菜，另一个人说故乡会添香料。谈话沿着米粒走远，又回到眼前的鸡蛋。大家各说各家的做法，筷子却都在同一阵锅香里停不下来。

**Egg Fried Rice**

Rice loosens in the hot wok, egg turns gold and spring onion rises with the spatula. The inn's egg fried rice has an easy way of starting conversations about home.

**Between the grains**

Cooked rice must separate while keeping its fragrance. Some cooks coat each grain with egg; others prefer tender pieces. Heat, seasoning and familiar gestures give a humble plate many recognisable versions, each remembered by somebody.

**Three turns of the spatula**

During a rush, the plate arrives promptly and still steaming. Its fragrance can turn a passer-by from the sign toward the doorway. A traveller eats, and the bag on their shoulder gradually settles beside the chair.

**Your family's version**

Peas, yesterday's vegetables, a favourite spice: memories cross the table. The conversation travels outward with the grains, then returns to the egg and wok fragrance in front of everyone.

### 酸梅汤 · Suān méi tāng

乌梅的酸香沉在杯底，甜味随后轻轻回转。酸梅汤是小馆常备的饮品，热菜吃到一半，端起它，舌尖便换了一阵清风。

**梅香有深浅**

酸梅汤常以乌梅调味，熏制的梅香带一点幽深，酸与甜在杯中彼此牵引。人家各有添减，有的偏酸，有的留甘。杯色深浅之外，更值得等的是咽下之后慢慢回来的那一缕滋味。

**杯沿留一口**
